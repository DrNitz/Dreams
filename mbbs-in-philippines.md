## 1. Why students choose it

There are four honest reasons, and one bad one.

The first is language. Lectures, textbooks, hospital charts and patients are all in English. In Russia or China you spend the first years learning a language just to understand a ward round. Here you start as a medical student on day one.

The second is clinical access. Philippine hospitals are busy, and students are allowed to examine patients, assist in deliveries and stand in theatre from the clinical years. In several other destinations foreign students watch. Watching is not the same as doing, and the difference shows up when you sit an exam that tests clinical judgement.

The third is the shape of the degree. It follows the American model: a four-year MD after a bachelor's, with the internship built into the programme. You graduate with a Doctor of Medicine, which NMC treats as equivalent to MBBS for Indian registration.

The fourth is distance and culture. Flights from India run five to six hours, the climate is tropical, and Filipino families are warm to Indian students in a way that makes the first year survivable. Cebu in particular feels less like a foreign country than like a very different Indian city.

The bad reason is that it looks cheap in a brochure. It is cheaper than a private Indian medical seat. It is not cheap, and it is not a shortcut. Treat it as a long, expensive, serious course of study that happens to be abroad.

## 2. The degree, and how long it takes

You graduate with a **Doctor of Medicine (MD)**. In the Philippines that is the primary medical degree, not a postgraduate one. NMC recognises it as equivalent to MBBS for the purpose of Indian registration, provided the college is on the list.

The programme runs about **five and a half years including internship**. The first two years are classroom and laboratory: anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, then pathology, pharmacology, microbiology and the rest. Third year onwards you are in the hospital. The final year is a rotating internship.

If you take the BSBio pre-medical year first, add six months to a year. That route exists mainly for students who still need to clear NEET, and [it has its own page](https://drdreams.in/without-neet.md).

One warning about duration. Curricula change, and NMC's requirements have tightened. A college that quotes you four years is either describing an old programme or a compressed one that will cause you problems at registration. Ask for the current curriculum in writing.

## 3. Recognition in India

This is the section to read twice.

Your degree is useful in India if, and only if, the college appears on the National Medical Commission's list of recognised foreign medical institutions. Not "will be listed soon". Not "in process". On the list, when you enrol, and when you graduate.

Check two places before you pay anything:

- The NMC's own list, at [nmc.org.in](https://www.nmc.org.in).

- The World Directory of Medical Schools, at [search.wdoms.org](https://search.wdoms.org). NMC's 2026 notice leans on this directory for verification.

Then there is the part nobody puts in the brochure. After the MD, you sit **FMGE**, which is being replaced by **NExT** over time, and you complete a **twelve month internship in India** before you register with a state medical council. The exam is hard, and the students who fail are usually the ones who planned to start preparing after graduation. [More on both exams](https://drdreams.in/fmge-next.md).

The 2026 rules added detail on the 54 month requirement, compensation for any online teaching, and internship verification. [The full notice, with the official PDF](https://drdreams.in/mci-rules.md), is worth an hour of your time before you commit.

## 5. What it costs

For the whole programme, tuition plus hostel plus food plus flights plus visa renewals, most families land between **Rs 15 lakh and Rs 25 lakh**. The spread comes from the college, the city, and whether you cook or eat out.

A private Indian medical seat, by comparison, is quoted in tens of lakhs for tuition alone, before donation. That gap is why this option exists at all. It is not a small amount of money, and a quote that presents it as cheap is worth checking line by line.

The [cost page](https://drdreams.in/fees-and-cost.md) breaks the total into each component and compares it with Russia, Georgia, China and Kazakhstan.

## 6. NEET and the BSBio year

NMC requires NEET for Indian students who intend to practise in India. That is the rule, and nothing waives it.

What exists is a workaround in timing, not in requirement. You enrol in BSBio, a pre-medical biology year at a Philippine university, without NEET. While you study, you prepare for and sit NEET. Once you clear it, you move into the MD at the same university. You lose six months to a year, and you gain a year of English-medium science teaching and a soft landing in a new country.

Students who have already done a year of serious NEET coaching usually skip BSBio and go straight to the MD, provided their NEET score qualifies them. [The detailed version is here](https://drdreams.in/without-neet.md), including what BSBio does and does not fix.

## 7. Getting in

The short version: pick the college, email its international office, send documents, get the acceptance letter, apply for the visa, fly. There is no entrance exam of the kind you are imagining, and there is no lottery.

The documents are ones you already have or can obtain in a week: 10th and 12th marksheets, passport, birth certificate, police clearance, medical fitness certificate, photographs. The [process page](https://drdreams.in/admission-process.md) lists them with the order of operations and the visa steps.

The process is mostly email and patience. The one stage where paying someone is reasonable is the visa file, if your documents are messy or you have a refusal on record, because a rejected application costs a semester.

## 8. What the week looks like

Classes in the morning, lab or ward in the afternoon, and then the real work: figuring out dinner. Food is the thing every Indian student underestimates. Cebu has Indian restaurants and spice shops, but not on every corner. Learn to cook three things before you leave.

The accent takes about a month to tune into. The heat takes about a week. The loneliness takes longer, and it is the reason to pick a college with a decent Indian batch if you are the kind of person who needs company. [Food, safety, housing and money, in detail](https://drdreams.in/student-life.md).

## 9. The four expensive mistakes

- **Paying a deposit before checking the college.** Check the NMC list first. It takes two minutes and it is free.

- **Choosing on first-year fees alone.** A cheap first year with expensive later years is a sales technique, not a discount.

- **Starting FMGE preparation in the final year.** By then the subjects you need are two years behind you. Start in first year, slowly.

- **Believing the college will handle your registration.** The college handles its paperwork. Yours is yours, and NMC's verification is strict about the internship and the 54 month requirement.

## 10. A timeline for the 2027 intake

Months move between colleges, so treat this as a shape rather than a schedule, and confirm dates with the admissions office.

1. **Now to February.** Shortlist two or three colleges. Check each on the NMC and WDOMS lists. Get the current fee sheet from each in writing.

2. **February to April.** Prepare documents. Sit NEET if you have not. Speak to students in your shortlisted colleges before you choose.

3. **April to June.** Apply. Receive the acceptance letter. Start the visa file.

4. **June to August.** Visa, flight, hostel reservation, first month of money in a Philippine bank account once you land.

5. **August onwards.** Enrolment and the first semester. Join or form a study group early, because the FMGE clock starts now, not in year four.

The one line version

Pick a college that is on the NMC list, confirm the total cost in writing, learn to cook, and start FMGE preparation in first year. Everything else is detail, and the detail is on the pages linked above.

Questions

## What families ask before they commit

How long does MBBS take in the Philippines?

About five and a half years including internship. Add six months to a year if you take the BSBio pre-medical route. Confirm the current curriculum with the college in writing, because it has changed before.

Is NEET required to study medicine in the Philippines?

NMC requires NEET for Indian students who intend to practise in India. Some colleges admit students to BSBio without it, and the student clears NEET before the MD. Going without NEET and expecting to practise in India is the mistake, not the BSBio year.

Is the Philippines better than Russia or China?

The teaching is in English, patients speak English, and foreign students get hands on clinical work, which is not true in every destination. The trade off is cost and distance from home. Whether it is better depends on your budget and how you handle being far away, not on a ranking.

What happens after the MD?

You sit FMGE or NExT, complete a twelve month internship in India, then register with the state medical council. Plan for that exam from the first year, not the last. [Both exams explained](https://drdreams.in/fmge-next.md).
