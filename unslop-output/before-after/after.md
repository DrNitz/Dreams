# First Year in Cebu: Food, Travel, Rent, and Real Money

I spent five and a half years in Cebu for my MD. The first month is never about anatomy—it is about heat, food, rain, and figuring out which street vendor won't send you to the clinic. Here is the unfiltered ground reality before you fly.

## The Money: 28 to 35 Lakh All-In
Do not believe an agency if they say you can finish everything for 18 or 20 Lakhs. The realistic total budget for 5.5 to 6 years is **28 Lakh to 35 Lakh INR**. 
- That figure covers your actual college tuition, visa renewal fees, apartment rent, food, and day-to-day living.
- **Agency fee rule**: Never pay the full course fee to a consultancy upfront. Always pay in installments. The consultancy only handles your 1st-year BS Biology package (documentation, Delhi MEA attestation, police clearance, visa, books, and flight). From year two onward, you pay tuition directly to the college cashier at standard rates.
- When you fly, ensure your ticket is an actual round-trip flight or an agency return dummy ticket. Philippine immigration will turn you back at the border without proof of return.

## Food: Rice, Pork, and Why You Need an Induction Stove
Filipino food is rice-based, sweet, heavy on pork, and rarely spicy. In your first semester, you will eat rice and fried eggs more times than you care to admit.
- Local dishes you can actually eat: *Chicken Adobo* (cooked in vinegar, garlic, and soy sauce) and *Chicken Curry* (tastes similar to home, just with milder masala).
- Indian messes run near Southwestern and Gullas, but they cost more.
- What to pack from India: Pack 5 to 8 kg of essential spices—turmeric, cumin, coriander powder, garam masala, and red chilli powder—in your checked baggage. Spices are scarce and overpriced in Cebu supermarkets.
- Buy a cheap rice cooker and an induction stove during your first week. Cooking dal and rice with your flatmates is how you keep your monthly food cost under 8,000 Pesos.

## Transport: Angkas, Maxim, and Jeepneys
You do not need taxis every day. Unmarked white taxis at night will overcharge you.
- Download **Angkas** and **Maxim** on your phone as soon as you get your local SIM. They are bike taxis with fixed app pricing, safety helmets, and the fastest way through Cebu rush-hour traffic between Banilad, Colon, and Urgello.
- **Jeepneys**: The cheapest way to move around (12 to 15 Pesos per ride). Learn the route codes (like 04L or 17B) that pass your campus within your first two weeks.
- Use **Grab** when you are carrying groceries from Ayala Center or traveling in a group at night.
- For deliveries and moving parcels, use **Shopee** for online essentials and **Lalamove** for moving luggage between flats.

## Weather and Housing
Cebu is hot and humid year-round, with heavy typhoons and street flooding from June to November.
- Do not bring expensive woolen jackets. Bring lightweight cotton shirts and buy one sturdy, windproof umbrella that costs 400 Pesos. Cheap 100-Peso street umbrellas will snap on your second rainy afternoon.
- Stay in a college-affiliated hostel for your first two semesters. It has curfews and basic bunk beds, but it keeps you secure while you learn the neighborhoods. Move into an apartment with two trusted classmates in second year once you understand local utility billing and lease deposits.

The real danger in Cebu is not crime—Numbeo crime figures put Cebu on par with major Indian metros, and locals are genuinely friendly to Indian students. The actual danger is losing your discipline, roaming the beaches, and failing the FMGE with less than 150 marks after 5 years. Stay focused on the books from month one.
