What is MBBS in the Philippines called?

The degree is a Doctor of Medicine, or MD. It is the Philippine equivalent of the Indian MBBS and it is what Indian students mean when they say MBBS in the Philippines. Filipino colleges also run a separate four-year pre-medical bachelor's degree, usually BS Biology, which their own students take before the MD.

The MD runs five and a half years including the internship, is taught in English, and is open to foreign students who have finished class 12 with Physics, Chemistry and Biology. On paper it is a postgraduate degree. In practice, for an Indian student arriving after class 12, it is a single continuous programme from first year to internship. The full guide to the degree.

How long does MBBS take in the Philippines?

Five and a half years: four years of classroom and clinical study, then a twelve-month internship in a hospital attached to the same university. Students who take the BS Biology year first add another year.

NMC's Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate Regulations require the foreign course to run at least 54 months of academic study, excluding internship, and the internship to be completed at the same institution. A 5.5-year MD satisfies both if you finish on time. The whole thing has to be completed within ten years of joining, including any repeated year, so a lost year is survivable but not free. What does MBBS in the Philippines cost?

Between Rs 28 lakh and Rs 35 lakh for the whole programme, for most students at the six colleges on this site. College fees are Rs 20 lakh to Rs 24 lakh of that, and hostel, food, flights and renewals make up the rest.

Those lines are hostel, food, flights twice a year, visa renewals, health insurance, FMGE coaching where it is not included, and a buffer for the emergency nobody plans for. The cheapest college and the most expensive one differ by about eight lakh over five and a half years. That is a real gap, but it is smaller than most quotes suggest, because flights and living costs are the same wherever you go. Line by line, with a worksheet.

Is MBBS from the Philippines valid in India?

Yes, if the college is on India's NMC list when you enrol and when you graduate, and if you clear FMGE or NExT and then complete a twelve-month internship in India. Skip any of those and the degree will not get you registered to practise.

The NMC list is the whole test. A college that is not on it produces a degree India will not recognise, whatever a brochure says. Check the list yourself on the NMC site and cross-check the college on WDOMS, then keep the screenshot with the date on it. The six colleges, with their NMC status.

What are the latest NMC rules for Philippines graduates?

The rules that matter are the Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate Regulations 2021 and the NMC public notice of 5 March 2026. Together they require a course of at least 54 months, a twelve-month internship at the same foreign university, WDOMS listing, and a twelve-month compulsory rotating internship in India after you clear FMGE or NExT.

Can I study MBBS in the Philippines without NEET?

You can start, but you cannot finish and practise in India without it. Some colleges admit students to the BS Biology year without NEET, and the student clears NEET before moving into the MD. NMC requires NEET for Indian students who want to practise in India, with no exceptions.

The BS Biology route is useful for a student who missed the exam or scored too low for a seat, and it costs an extra year and about three to four lakh rupees. It buys time to prepare. It does not replace the exam. How the BS Biology route works.

Which colleges in the Philippines are taking admissions for 2027?

All six colleges covered here are accepting applications for the 2027 intake. Philippine medical colleges run one main intake a year, with classes beginning in August and September, and applications stay open through the spring until seats fill.

The practical deadline is not the application form. It is the visa appointment and the document authentication, both of which take weeks. A student who applies in June for an August start is usually too late. February to May is the working window. Compare the six, including fees and hospital size.

What documents do I need to apply?

Class 10 and 12 marksheets, NEET scorecard, passport with eighteen months of validity, birth certificate, medical certificate, police clearance, passport photographs, proof of funds, and the college's application form with a short personal statement.

The birth certificate and marksheets usually need notarisation, state attestation and a Ministry of External Affairs apostille before the embassy will accept them, which takes two to four weeks. The full checklist with formats.

Is it safe to study MBBS in the Philippines?

For a student living in a hostel near campus, in Cebu, Manila or Davao, yes. The bigger risks are traffic, petty theft and bad decisions at two in the morning, not violence against foreign students. Thousands of Indian students have lived there for years without incident.

Practical rules that actually keep you safe: live within walking distance of campus or on a well-lit main road, use Grab instead of unlicensed taxis at night, do not carry a laptop in a backpack on a jeepney, keep your documents in a hostel safe, and tell someone where you are going. The cities are not dangerous in the way the news makes them sound. They are not so safe that you can be careless, either. What daily life is actually like.

Is the course taught in English?

Yes. Lectures, textbooks, examinations and hospital records are in English, and English is one of the two official languages of the Philippines. Filipino and Bisaya are spoken in corridors and canteens.

The first month is hard anyway. Professors speak English with a Filipino accent, patients mix in Bisaya, and the speed takes getting used to. Most students understand two thirds of the first lectures and everything by the end of the first semester. Nobody fails for the accent. More on the language problem.

Do I need to learn Filipino or Bisaya?

No. You can complete the degree in English. Learning a hundred words of Bisaya or Tagalog will change your hospital experience, though, and patients will treat you differently for trying.

Clinical years are where it matters. A patient who understands your question answers it. A patient who does not gives you a nod and a guess. Ordering food, asking directions and haggling in the market are the other places a little language goes a long way.

Is FMGE compulsory after I come back?

Yes, if you want to practise in India. Foreign medical graduates sit FMGE or, once it is fully in place, NExT, then complete a twelve-month internship in India, then register with a state medical council.

The pass rate is low, in the range of one in five to one in four in most sessions. Most of that is timing: students start preparing in final year. The ones who clear it begin in first year, with Indian textbooks alongside the Philippine ones. A study plan by year.

Can I work in the Philippines after graduating?

Yes, since November 2026. The Philippine Professional Regulation Commission now permits Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools to sit the Physician Licensure Examination (PLE) and register to practise in the country. The Embassy of India in Manila confirmed the change. Before this, Philippine law required India to give Filipino doctors the same right, and it did not, so the route was closed.

The PLE gives you a licence to practise in the Philippines. It does not register you in India: to work here you still sit FMGE or NExT and complete the twelve month internship, whichever route you take. If you want to stay, prepare for the PLE the way you prepare for FMGE, from first year. Confirm the current eligibility with the Professional Regulation Commission and your college before you rely on it. How the Philippine licence works.

What is the difference between MD in the Philippines and MBBS in India?

On paper, the Philippine MD is a postgraduate degree and the Indian MBBS is an undergraduate one. In practice both are five and a half years with an internship, both are taught in English, and both lead to a licence only after a qualifying exam and internship in the country where you want to work.

The real differences are clinical and cultural. Philippine hospitals let students do hands-on work early, which Indian colleges often do not. The trade is that you do your internship in the Philippines and then repeat a year of internship in India after FMGE, so the whole path is longer than the Indian one by about a year. How much does living in the Philippines cost per month?

Less than most Indian cities for rent, more for groceries. A shared or hostel room, food, transport, phone data and the occasional night out come to a modest monthly figure, and the hostel is where students either save or overspend.

In Cebu and Davao a basic hostel is cheap and a comfortable one is not. Manila costs more for everything except food, and the traffic costs you time you will not get back. Cooking for yourself is the single biggest saving, and the reason a college with an Indian mess works out cheaper than its tuition suggests. The full cost breakdown.

When should I start applying for the 2027 intake?

Start in December or January for an August 2027 start. By February you should know your shortlist, by March you should have the college's conditional acceptance, and by May your visa file should be at the embassy.

The order that works: check NMC and WDOMS first, email the colleges, get the document list in writing, start authentication while you wait, sit NEET if you have not, then apply for the visa as soon as the acceptance letter arrives. Every year a batch of students misses the intake because they began in June. The step-by-step timeline.

Can my parents visit me during the course?

Yes. They can apply for a visitor visa, and most colleges will issue an invitation letter if the admissions office is asked in advance. Flights are cheapest booked two to three months out, and December and January are the expensive weeks.

A visit in the first semester is worth more than it sounds. Parents who have seen the hostel, met the warden and eaten in the mess stop worrying, and students who are not managing a worried phone call every night do better in first year.

What happens if I fail a subject or lose a year?

You repeat the subject, usually in a summer term or with the next batch, and you pay for the repeat. The degree does not disappear. What you cannot do is stretch the whole programme past ten years from the date you joined, because NMC will not recognise a course that took longer.

One repeated subject is common and recoverable. Three lost years is a different problem. If you are failing, the fix is almost always the same: fewer hours in the hostel, more hours in the library, and an honest conversation with the faculty before the exam rather than after it.

Can Indian graduates take the Philippine PLE now?

Yes, since November 2026. The Philippine Professional Regulation Commission permits Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools to sit the Physician Licensure Examination and register to practise in the Philippines. The Embassy of India, Manila confirmed the change.

The PLE licences you in the Philippines, not in India. To practise in India you still sit FMGE or NExT and complete the twelve month Indian internship. The licence matters if you want the option to stay and work in the Philippines, or if you want your file to satisfy every NMC condition while you are there. How the licence is obtained.

What is the BSBio route, and does it replace NEET?

The BSBio is a pre-medical bachelor's degree, usually BS Biology, that a student straight out of class 12 takes before the MD. It does not replace NEET. NMC still requires NEET for an Indian student who wants to register and practise in India.

The Philippine MD is a postgraduate course: it needs a bachelor's degree and the NMAT. The BSBio year gives you both the degree and time to prepare for NEET. If you take it, get the transfer conditions in writing, including which credits count and how many years it adds. The route in full.
