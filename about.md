About

# Written by Dr. Nitz

I'm Nitish, and Dr. Nitz is the name I write under. I finished the Doctor of Medicine at Southwestern University in Cebu in 2025. These pages are what I know about studying medicine in the Philippines, written down for the student I was in 2019.

By Dr. Nitz. Last updated 22 November 2026.

## The short version

DrDreams is a set of reference pages about studying medicine in the Philippines, written for Indian students and their parents. It covers recognition, cost, the documents, admission, the exams afterwards, and what the years in between actually feel like.

The information that decides a five-year, twenty-lakh decision is scattered across regulator PDFs, college websites written for a different audience, and group chats where half the answers are wrong. This site puts it in one place, in plain English, with the source for anything that can be checked.

Some of it is first hand. Some comes from classmates, graduates and students I have stayed in touch with. Each page says which, in a line near the top, so you can weigh it.

## One graduate, five and a half years

I landed in Cebu in June 2019 with two suitcases and a folder of documents I had checked four times. The first year was hostel food, a fan that clicked all night, and understanding about two thirds of every lecture. By second year, Cebu was a place I lived in rather than a place I was stuck in.

I finished the MD in 2025 and cleared FMGE on the first attempt. Then I came home to the same question every batch asks: where do I start? Most of these pages are the answer I wish someone had sent me as a PDF.

I am not a professor, a cardiologist or an admissions officer. I am one graduate who kept every fee receipt, read the NMC notices properly, and still remembers what the paperwork felt like at nineteen. Where a page goes beyond that, it says so and links the source.

Method

## How a page gets made

1.

### Start with the primary source

Recognition comes from the [NMC](https://www.nmc.org.in) list and [WDOMS](https://search.wdoms.org). Fees come from the college's own fee sheet for the intake. Exam rules come from NMC and NBEMS notices, not from a coaching centre's summary or a remembered version of the rules. If a claim cannot be traced to one of those, it does not go on the page as fact.

2.

### Say what is wrong with it

Every college page carries cons in the same type as the pros: the size of the Indian batch, the hospital, the hostel, the city, the things graduates complain about when the brochure is closed. A page with no cons is an advertisement, and there are enough of those already.

3.

### Mark what is experience and what is not

Some pages come from five and a half years in Cebu. Others come from graduates and students who studied somewhere I did not. Each one says which, in a line near the top, so you can weigh it. Second-hand detail is useful. Pretending it is first-hand is not.

4.

### Date it, then leave the door open

Rules and fees move. Every page carries the month it was last reviewed, and the review date changes when the content does. Corrections are welcome from anyone, including a college that thinks its page is unfair, and they are checked against the source rather than against who asked loudest.

What these pages do not claim

- That a seat, a visa, an internship or a licence is guaranteed. None of them are.

- That a college is good because it is on the NMC list. The list means recognition, not quality.

- That a fee quoted here will still be the fee when you pay. Fee sheets change every intake, which is why the pages tell you what to ask for.

- That this is medical, legal or immigration advice. It is information from one graduate, and the regulator and the college are the authority.

- Any connection to NMC, NBEMS, CHED, the Philippine government or any college named here.

When a page is wrong, it gets corrected and the correction is dated. That is the whole editorial policy, and the [contact page](https://drdreams.in/contact.md) is how it starts.

Why the site exists

## The account that started it

Everything above is policy. This is the part that explains the policy, and it is the only personal account on the site. It is mine.

"NEET got me a qualification and not a government seat. I applied to Southwestern University in Cebu myself: emailed the admissions office, sent my own documents, and hired a consultant for the embassy paperwork alone. That saved money my family did not have to spare."

"The first year was hard in ways nobody had warned me about. The food, the accent, learning to cook badly. By second year Cebu was a place I lived in rather than a place I was stuck in."

"FMGE I started in second year and did it badly, then got serious in final year. Six to eight hours a day, past papers, a small group I studied with. First attempt."

Dr. Nitz, MD, Southwestern University, Cebu, 2025.

The first year is where most of the questions I get come from, and it is the part no brochure describes. So the site has a page for it, written from the inside rather than from a campus tour. [Life in the Philippines](https://drdreams.in/student-life.md) is the honest version, including the parts that were bad.

Studied in the Philippines?

If you have and want your account on this site, send it. Say whether it can carry your first name, your college and your year. Nothing is edited into praise: the complaints are the useful part.
