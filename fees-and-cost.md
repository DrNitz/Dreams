# MBBS in the Philippines fees: what five years really cost

Tuition is the number everyone quotes. Tuition plus hostel plus food plus flights plus the visa renewal is the number that empties the account. Here is the whole thing, line by line.

*Reviewed 26 November 2026. Fee sheets are the 2026-2027 versions published by the colleges.*

**Why the published sheets live on the college pages.**

Four of the six colleges have published 2026-2027 fee sheets, and they are on each college page and summarised below. Sheets change every intake, sometimes twice a year, so treat them as a starting point and confirm the current sheet in writing before you pay.

What this page can do is tell you exactly which lines to ask for, and how they add up. That is worth more than a number that ages badly.

*The lines*

## Eight things you will pay for

Ask for each of these separately, in writing, from the college. A quote that cannot be broken into these lines is either disorganised or hiding something.

1. ### Tuition, per year

   The biggest line, and the one that rises. Ask for the fee for each year of the programme, not just year one. Some colleges hold it flat, some do not.
2. ### Hostel

   Per month or per semester. Confirm whether air conditioning, electricity and water are included. In Cebu and Davao, a basic hostel is cheap; a comfortable one is not.
3. ### Food

   An Indian mess is the cheapest option where one exists. Where it does not, you are cooking, and groceries in the Philippines are not as cheap as in India. This line is where the cheapest college turns expensive.
4. ### Flights, twice a year

   Manila and Cebu are easier and cheaper to reach than Davao. Multiply by five years. It is a real line, not a rounding error.
5. ### Visa and immigration costs

   The student visa, then renewals, then the annual registration with Philippine immigration. These recur every year and are almost never in the quote you get in India.
6. ### Health insurance

   Usually required, usually annual, and often forgotten until enrolment week.
7. ### FMGE coaching

   Included at some colleges, separate at others, and not offered at all at one. If it is separate, price an online Indian course before you choose, because you will need one.
8. ### A buffer

   One emergency flight home, one medical bill, one laptop replacement. Every batch has a student who needed it. Put it in the budget from the start.

Hostel and food is the biggest variable in the total. The packaged rate on those sheets is 14,000 to 15,000 pesos a month. Sharing a room and cooking brings it to 8,000 to 10,000, and over sixty months that difference is a few lakh rupees, which is why two students at the same college can spend very different amounts.

*The enrolment fee is not refunded at any of the four. Visa conversion and extension in the Philippines are paid by the student at actual cost. The internship fee is discussed at the time of internship at DMSF, Jose Maria and Brokenshire.*

## Against the other destinations

These are the ranges quoted to students we have spoken to, and what students from those countries reported. Treat them as a starting point for your own calls, not as prices.

Indicative ranges and the practical differences, not a price list.

| Destination | Typically quoted | Language of teaching | The real difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Philippines** | Rs 28 to 35 lakh | English | Clinical access for foreign students, tropical climate, 5 to 6 hour flight |
| Russia | Rs 20 to 35 lakh | Russian in the early years | Cold winters, limited patient contact for foreign students |
| Georgia | Rs 25 to 40 lakh | English, heavy accent | High cost, small Indian community, cold winters |
| China | Rs 20 to 30 lakh | Mandarin by the clinical years | Language barrier in hospitals, very different food culture |
| Kazakhstan | Rs 18 to 25 lakh | Russian and Kazakh | Fewer Indian students, little FMGE coaching infrastructure |
| Ukraine | Not recommended now | Ukrainian and Russian | Ongoing conflict. Students have had to leave mid-course |

The point of the table is not that the Philippines wins. It is that the cheapest headline is rarely the cheapest degree, and the language of instruction changes your entire clinical training. A destination where you cannot speak to a patient is not cheaper, it is a different course.

*Worth paying for*

## Where money is well spent

- **A visa consultant, if the file is messy.** One rejection costs a semester. A licensed consultant who has processed Philippine student visas before earns the fee in that one case.
- **A good hostel in year one.** Security and a short commute matter more than saving two thousand pesos a month.
- **An FMGE course from first year.** The cheapest insurance you will ever buy against repeating an exam.
- **Health insurance.** Non-negotiable. One hospital admission without it can cost more than a year of premiums.

*Not worth paying for*

## Where money disappears

- **Cash paid without a receipt.** Fees go to the institution and come back on college letterhead. If the receipt is a handwritten note, it is not a receipt.
- **"Documentation charges" on top of a quote.** Photocopies and couriers cost a few hundred rupees. Anything larger needs an itemised bill.
- **Deposits paid before an acceptance letter exists.** The letter is the only thing that proves a seat. Pay after it, not before it.
- **Two quotes that do not match.** Ask both for the same eight lines. The gap between them is the part nobody explained.

**Before any transfer.**

No college in the Philippines needs money sent to an individual's personal account. Fees go to the institution, and you get a receipt on college letterhead. If the account name is a person, stop and ask why in writing.

*Questions*

## The money questions, answered plainly

**What is the total cost of MBBS in the Philippines?**

Most families spend between roughly Rs 28 lakh and Rs 35 lakh across the whole programme. College fees are Rs 20 lakh to Rs 24 lakh of that, and hostel, food, flights and visa renewals make up the rest. The spread comes from the college, the city and whether the student cooks. DMSF sits above it, around Rs 38 lakh, because its tuition is quoted in dollars.

Ask each college for a current fee sheet rather than relying on any published figure, including this one.

**Is it cheaper than a private medical college in India?**

The tuition is lower than a private Indian medical seat, which is often quoted in tens of lakhs before any donation. It is not cheap in absolute terms, and the living costs of five and a half years abroad are real. Compare the all-in total, not the first-year fee.

**What costs get left out of a fee quote?**

Visa renewals, the annual registration with Philippine immigration, health insurance, hostel deposits, local fees payable in pesos, flights home, and FMGE coaching. Those six lines are where a quote that looked affordable turns into a budget problem in year two.

## Ask for the fee sheet

*Every college has a fee sheet for the intake. Ask for it in writing, with hostel and miscellaneous lines included. If someone quotes a single total instead, ask which lines it covers.*
