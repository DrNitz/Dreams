# After MBBS in the Philippines: what comes next

The degree is the middle of the story, not the end. Here is the order from graduation to registration, and the realistic timeline for each step.

## The order of events

Graduation is not the finish line. The route from a Philippine MD to an Indian white coat has four gates, and they happen in a fixed order:

1. Graduate with the MD from an NMC-listed college.

2. Pass FMGE or NExT.

3. Complete a twelve month compulsory rotating internship in India.

4. Register with a state medical council.

You cannot swap the order. You cannot do the Indian internship before the exam. You cannot register before the internship. And none of it fixes a college that was not listed when you joined. If you have not checked that yet, read [is the degree valid in India](https://drdreams.in/is-mbbs-in-philippines-valid-in-india.md) first.

## Come home and sit FMGE or NExT

FMGE is the Foreign Medical Graduate Examination, conducted by the National Board of Examinations in Medical Sciences. NExT, the National Exit Test, is being introduced and is expected to replace FMGE over time. Your batch will sit whichever exam applies when you graduate, so check the current NBEMS bulletin rather than an old blog post.

The exam covers the Indian MBBS syllabus: pre-clinical subjects, para-clinical subjects, and the clinical subjects. It tests whether you can think like an Indian intern. It does not test how hospitals in the Philippines run, which is why students who only study for their college exams struggle.

The practical preparation starts in first year. Keep Indian subject notes alongside your Philippine lectures. Use FMGE question banks slowly, not as a last-year cram. The [FMGE and NExT page](https://drdreams.in/fmge-next.md) has a year-by-year plan and the mistakes that cost people a year.

## The twelve month Indian internship

After passing the exam, you complete a twelve month compulsory rotating internship, usually called CRMI, at an approved institution in India. It rotates through medicine, surgery, obstetrics and gynaecology, paediatrics, community medicine and the allied departments.

Indian hospitals use this year to see whether your clinical training was hands-on. Students who spent five years watching from a corridor feel the difference in the first month. Students who clerked, presented and assisted in the Philippines settle in faster.

Apply early. Seats are limited, states have different processes, and the window between passing the exam and starting the internship can be months. Ask the institution and your state medical council for the current process in writing.

## Register with a state medical council

With the exam passed and the internship complete, you apply for registration with the state medical council where you intend to practise. Registration is the legal step that makes you a doctor in India. It is separate from the degree, the NMC list and the exam.

Keep the paper trail: degree, transcript, internship certificate, migration certificate, exam pass certificate, and the NMC and WDOMS screenshots from your enrolment year. Registration offices can ask for documents years later, and a college that has changed administration may be slow to reissue them.

## PG in India

If you want an MD or MS in India, the route is NEET-PG. Foreign graduates usually need to clear FMGE or NExT and complete the Indian internship before they are eligible, but the eligibility rules are set in the current NEET-PG information bulletin. Read it for your year, not a summary from a coaching centre.

PG preparation is a different exam from FMGE. FMGE tests the undergraduate syllabus; NEET-PG tests applied clinical decisions in more detail. Most students take a year or two of internship and preparation before sitting it. Starting early is an advantage, but burning out in first year is not.

## Jobs after registration

Once registered, the ordinary options are:

- **Hospital medical officer.** Private and charitable hospitals hire registered doctors for ward duty, emergency and outpatient work.

- **Primary care and clinics.** General practice, family clinics and community health centres.

- **Government health schemes.** State health departments and national programmes hire medical officers on contract and permanent roles.

- **Corporate and insurance medicine.** Medical officers for companies, hospitals and health insurers, including claims and hospital review work.

- **Clinical research and medical writing.** A less clinical path that values a medical degree and clear writing.

- **Hospital administration and public health.** Usually after an additional degree or diploma.

None of these are guaranteed by the degree. They are the normal destinations for a registered Indian doctor, and the competition is the same as for any other graduate.

## Options abroad

A Philippine MD is also a starting point for licensing routes elsewhere. Each has its own exam, fees and eligibility rules, and each should be checked at the official source for your year.

- **United States:** USMLE steps and ECFMG certification. The college's WDOMS listing matters.

- **United Kingdom:** PLAB and GMC registration.

- **Australia:** AMC examinations and registration.

- **Canada:** MCC examinations and provincial licensing.

- **Philippines:** practising there has its own licensure route and residency rules. Check with the Philippine Regulatory Commission and the college.

These routes are expensive and slow, and they are not a fallback for a failed FMGE. Decide early if one of them is your plan, because the exam preparation starts years before graduation.

## If you do not clear the exam

It happens, and it is not the end of your medical career. The first step is to check the current attempt rules and eligibility window in the NBEMS bulletin, because they determine what you can do next. The second is to diagnose why you failed: not enough clinical exposure, weak pre-clinical foundations, exam technique, or simply starting too late.

Then build a plan with a date. A repeat attempt with a fixed schedule, question bank targets and weekly tests is very different from "I will study harder". If you are working while preparing, protect two uninterrupted hours a day and one long test every week. The students who clear on a second attempt usually change the method, not just the hours.

## A realistic timeline

| Stage | Typical time | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Graduate and collect documents | 1 to 3 months | Degree, transcript, internship certificate, migration certificate |
| Prepare and sit FMGE or NExT | 6 to 12 months | Indian syllabus, question banks, weekly tests |
| Apply for the Indian internship | 1 to 3 months | State council and institution applications |
| Complete CRMI | 12 months | Rotations, logbook, certificate |
| Register | 1 to 3 months | State medical council application |

From graduation to registration, with the waiting time included.

The whole sequence usually takes two to three years after graduation. Plan for it financially before you leave for the Philippines, not after you return.

Questions

## After graduation, in plain answers

Can I work in India right after the MD?

No. You need to pass FMGE or NExT, complete the twelve month Indian internship, and register with a state medical council. Only then can you practise.

Can I do PG in India after a Philippine MD?

Yes, through NEET-PG, usually after clearing FMGE or NExT and completing the Indian internship. The eligibility rules are in the current NEET-PG information bulletin.

What if I fail FMGE?

Check the current attempt rules, find out why you failed, and build a fixed study plan with a date. Many doctors clear on a later attempt. It costs time and money, not your career.

Do I have to do the internship in India?

Yes, for Indian registration. The Philippine internship is part of the MD. The Indian CRMI is a separate twelve month requirement after the exam.

Can I practise in the Philippines instead?

Yes, since November 2026. The Philippine Professional Regulation Commission now permits Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools to sit the Physician Licensure Examination and register to practise in the country, which the Embassy of India in Manila confirmed. Before that, the reciprocity rule kept the route closed. The PLE licences you in the Philippines only. To practise in India you still sit FMGE or NExT and finish the twelve month internship.
