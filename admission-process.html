The full process

From documents in India to your first class

The paperwork starts at home and ends at enrolment. This page runs the whole sequence: what the college asks for, what the visa office asks for, what gets stamped, and what happens when you land.

Reviewed 25 November 2026. Written from my own application in 2019 and from what has changed in the paperwork since.

The documents
  • 10th and 12th marksheets and certificates
  • Passport, valid for well beyond your course
  • Birth certificate
  • Police clearance certificate
  • Medical fitness certificate
  • Passport-size photographs, white background
  • NEET scorecard, if you have one
The timeline

Allow three to four months from the first email to arrival. The acceptance letter is usually fast. The visa and the document gathering are the slow parts, and both get slower in the weeks before an intake. Once the visa file is complete, the office takes 20 to 25 days.

Start in February for a mid-year intake. Starting in May is how students end up paying express fees for a passport.

Before the MD, two requirements

The Philippine Doctor of Medicine is a postgraduate course. Enrolment needs a bachelor's degree and a pass in the National Medical Admission Test (NMAT). A student straight out of class 12 completes the pre-medical bachelor's degree first, which is the BSBio route, and that degree must itself be accredited by CHED.

Seven steps

The sequence

  1. Verify the college before you write to it

    Two lists: the NMC list and WDOMS. This takes two minutes and saves five years. My six are checked, but check them yourself anyway.

  2. Email the international admissions office

    Use the address on the college's own website, not a number forwarded on WhatsApp. Ask three things: the current fee sheet for the intake, the document list, and whether they have a BSBio route. If you need that route, ask for the transfer conditions in writing.

  3. Send the documents and get the acceptance letter

    The letter names you and the course. This is the document the embassy, the airline and the immigration officer will all want to see. Keep the original, carry a folder of copies.

  4. Apply for the student visa

    The college issues a support letter. You apply at the Philippine embassy or consulate in India with that letter, your acceptance letter, your passport, photographs and the financial documents in the visa checklist. Once the file is complete, the visa office takes 20 to 25 days. Keep every receipt. This is the one stage where a good consultant earns the fee, because a rejection costs a semester.

  5. Book the flight, reserve the first month of housing

    Book direct with the airline. Reserve a hostel through the student affairs office before you land. Look at apartments after you arrive and know the city, the commute and the neighbourhood.

  6. Arrive, enrol, and register with immigration

    Most colleges arrange airport pickup for international students. Enrolment is straightforward if your documents are complete. Then there is the local registration and, each year, a renewal. Put the renewal date in your phone the day you get it.

  7. Survive the first month

    SIM card, bank account, grocery run, and finding out which canteen is edible. This is the month where most students want to go home. The life page covers it in detail.

Who does what

Three parties, four decisions

Most of this process is email and patience. Three parties are involved and each has a fixed job. The four decisions in the middle are yours, and they are the ones that change the outcome.

The four decisions

  • Which college, and whether it is on the NMC list at the time you enrol
  • Whether the total cost fits, hostel and visa renewals included
  • Whether you can live in that city for five and a half years
  • When FMGE preparation starts. Year two is the honest answer

Where outside help is reasonable

  • The college issues the acceptance letter, the visa support letter and the enrolment papers
  • The embassy issues the 9(F) student visa once your file is complete
  • You gather the documents, hold the passport and sit the interview

The stages run in a fixed order: enquiry, documents, acceptance letter, visa support letter, visa appointment, flight. Each stage has one thing that can stall it, and almost always it is a document rather than a decision. Knowing what comes next is most of the work.

The timeline that works backwards from an August start is on the questions page, and the documents themselves are on the checklist page.

If you do it yourself

The four things that go wrong

  • A passport that expires mid-course. Check the validity before you apply. Renewing a passport from abroad is a slow, expensive annoyance.
  • Documents that are not apostilled or authenticated. Ask the college exactly which certificates need authentication and which do not. Guessing wastes a month.
  • Arriving before the hostel is ready. Confirm the move-in date in writing. Hotels in Cebu and Manila are not cheap when you are paying by the night.
  • Missing a visa renewal. Fines and a trip to immigration, and in a bad case a problem with your status. Set the reminder when you get the document, not when you remember.

The document checklist is the other half of this page.

Start with the passport

Everything else is replaceable. A passport with less than eighteen months of validity is not, because renewing it from the Philippines means weeks without your travel document. Check the expiry date before you do anything else on this page.

The admission file is the smaller one. Most of it is paperwork you already have in a drawer, plus one document you have to earn: the NEET scorecard. The visa file repeats about half of it and adds the financial papers. Neither list is long. Both are unforgiving about names, dates and stamps.

List one

What the college asks for

Colleges ask for these at different points. Some want scans before they issue the acceptance letter, some want attested hard copies on arrival. Ask which, in writing, so you are not carrying a folder across an ocean for nothing.

If you already hold a bachelor's degree, add the degree certificate and marksheets. If you studied outside India, add an equivalence statement from the Philippine school. Gap years need a short affidavit explaining them, and nobody will be scandalised by one.

Authentication

The stamp that takes the longest

A photocopy is not a document as far as the embassy is concerned. Indian certificates normally need three steps, in this order: notarisation, state attestation, then an apostille from the Ministry of External Affairs. Some states let you skip straight to the passport office route.

Budget three to four weeks. Tatkal exists, and it costs money. Do this while you are still choosing a college, because it is the slowest thing in the whole file and it has nothing to do with the college's speed.

One warning from experience: do not send originals anywhere until you have a complete set of attested photocopies in your own bag. Losing a birth certificate in transit is a two-month detour through your home district.

The order that works

  1. Passport renewed, if it expires within two years
  2. Name spelling identical on every document
  3. Marksheets and birth certificate notarised
  4. State attestation, then MEA apostille
  5. Scans sent to the college, hard copies in hand luggage
  6. Eight photocopies of everything, in a plastic folder

List two

What the visa office asks for

The student visa is a 9(F). The college issues a notice of acceptance and a support letter, and you take them to the Philippine embassy or consulate that covers your state. This is the document list the visa office hands out for the current intake. Confirm it with the college or the consulate before you submit, because the format rules are strict and the list changes.

  1. Passport

    The original, plus two colour copies.

  2. PCC status tracker

    A print of the tracker from the Passport Seva Portal.

  3. Police Clearance Certificate

    Through the Passport Seva Portal only. A PCC from a local police station is not accepted. Apply inside the stated window, and keep the original for submission.

  4. NEET scorecard

    Two colour copies.

  5. NEET admit card

    A black and white copy, as NTA issues it.

  6. Bank statement, last six months

    From the parent or sponsor's personal or salary account. A business account needs the GST registration certificate as well. Minimum closing balance Rs 3,00,000. Signed by the branch manager and sealed on every page, covering the full six months, with the balance showing at the time it was generated. An online statement without the signature and the seal is not accepted.

  7. Affidavit of financial support and no objection

    On Rs 100 bond paper, notarised. The same sponsor whose bank statement you submit signs it, and the notary is from the sponsor's district. The signature must match the PAN card, the later pages go on A4 green sheets, and the prescribed sample is followed.

  8. Photographs

    US visa size (2 x 2 inches, white background, about 80% face), five copies. Passport size, white background, five copies. US visa size with your name and the date on them, five copies.

  9. Medical test report

    From a NABH-accredited hospital only. Reports from pre-accreditation or NABL-accredited hospitals are not accepted. The report carries the medical officer's signature and the hospital seal, and your name must match the passport. Tests: chest X-ray (picture and report, A4), Hepatitis B (HBsAg), total blood examination, urine, stool, HIV and VDRL.

  10. Aadhaar card

    Colour copy. One for the student, one for the sponsor or parent whose bank statement and affidavit you submit.

  11. Mark sheets

    Class 10 and Class 12 originals, plus colour copies.

  12. Vaccination certificate, if you have one

    Download the International Travel Vaccination Certificate from CoWIN, with your passport number on it. If you were not vaccinated, ignore this one.

Four rules sit over the whole list. Every document must be genuine, complete and in the stated format. After you submit, the visa takes 20 to 25 days. A discrepancy, a fabricated document, a missing one or a badly prepared one delays the file. And the preparation is yours to do on time. Some colleges have students enter on a temporary visitor visa and convert after enrolment; others want the 9(F) before you fly. Ask which route your intake uses, because the two need different paperwork and different timing.

After you land

The documents that do not stop

The file does not close at immigration. In the first weeks the college helps you with the medical examination and the ACR I-Card, which is the identity card for foreign nationals. Keep eight photocopies of every page you have ever signed.

  • ACR I-Card. Applied for through the college after enrolment. You will need it for almost everything else, including a bank account.
  • Annual report. Foreign nationals file one every year within the first sixty days. Miss it and you pay a fine at the immigration office.
  • Visa renewal. Put the date in your phone the day the stamp is issued, with a reminder a month earlier.
  • NBI clearance. Needed later for hospital postings and for the internship year. It is a background check, not a college document.
Keep a digital copy

Scan everything to a cloud folder the day it is issued, and give a family member access. A lost file in Cebu is a week of emails. A lost file with no scans is a month of couriers.

Why files stall

Four mistakes, in order of frequency

  1. The name does not match

    Initials expanded on one document and not another, a missing middle name, a spelling the clerk corrected by hand. Fix it at the source. An affidavit of one name is cheaper than a rejected visa file.

  2. The passport expires mid-course

    Five and a half years is a long time. Renew first, then apply, even if it costs you a month at the start.

  3. The birth certificate is missing or unreadable

    If it was never registered, get it registered now. A delayed registration is accepted; a blank space in the file is not.

  4. The bank statement arrived yesterday

    Funds that appear the week before the interview look borrowed, because they usually are. Six months of ordinary movement is more convincing than a large deposit the day before.

Questions

About the paperwork

What bank balance does the student visa need?

The visa office asks for a six month statement from the parent or sponsor, with a closing balance of at least Rs 3,00,000. It has to be signed by the branch manager and sealed on every page. An online statement without those is rejected, and a business account also needs the GST registration certificate.

Deposits that appear the week before you apply read as borrowed. Six months of ordinary movement, ending above the minimum, is what the file is looking for.

Do my documents need an apostille for the Philippines?

Usually yes for the certificates the college forwards to the Philippine authorities, mainly the birth certificate and the class 12 marksheet. India and the Philippines are both part of the Apostille Convention, so a Ministry of External Affairs apostille is normally accepted.

Colleges differ on which documents they want authenticated and when, so ask the admissions office for the list in writing before you spend money on attestation.

How long does authentication take in India?

Plan for two to four weeks on the standard route: notary, then state Home Department or Regional Passport Office attestation, then the MEA apostille. Tatkal services in some states cut it to a week, at a price.

Start before the visa stage, not during it. This is the step that most often delays an intake.

Can I apply before my NEET result comes out?

Yes. Colleges issue conditional acceptance letters on the basis of your class 12 marks and your NEET roll number, and the scorecard is added when it arrives.

You cannot complete the visa file without it, because the embassy asks for the scorecard and NMC eligibility depends on the score.

What if my name is spelled differently on two documents?

Fix it before you apply. A mismatch between the passport, the marksheet and the birth certificate is the most common reason a file goes back, and it is much harder to correct from Cebu than from your own district office.

Get a corrected certificate or a one-name affidavit, then apply. Tell me if a college told you otherwise and I will look into it.

No NEET score

The BSBio route, and what it does not skip

Students ask whether they can start without NEET. There is a route, and it does not remove the exam. The version below is the short form; the FAQ answer covers the rest.

The Philippine Doctor of Medicine is a postgraduate degree. Enrolment needs a bachelor's degree and a pass in the National Medical Admission Test, the NMAT. A student straight out of class 12 does not have the bachelor's degree yet, so the college arranges a pre-medical year, usually a BS Biology, before the MD.

Not every provider runs the same length. Kings Academy in India offers a BSBio of its own that runs six months to a year, and colleges differ in what they will accept, so get the duration and the acceptance in writing before you pay for one.

That year is real study, not a waiting room. It buys time to prepare for NEET, and it gives you a Philippine bachelor's degree that CHED has to recognise. What it does not do is satisfy the NMC's NEET requirement for an Indian student who wants to register and practise in India.

The requirement sits on you, not the college

A Philippine college can admit you without a NEET score. The NMC can still refuse your registration later if you do not have one. Anyone who says the exam disappears is selling you a problem you will meet again in year five, in a room in India, with an officer asking for the scorecard.

If you take the BSBio route, get the transfer conditions in writing: which credits count, how many years it adds, and what happens if you clear NEET in the middle of it.

Questions

Before you start the paperwork

How long does the admission process take?

Allow three to four months from first email to arrival. The acceptance letter usually comes in two to four weeks. Document authentication and the visa appointment are what take the time, and both slow down in the weeks before an intake.

The document checklist covers the slowest part.

How long does the whole process take?

Allow three to four months from first email to arrival. The acceptance letter is usually quick. The visa and the document gathering are what take time, and both slow down in the weeks before an intake.

Can I apply to a Philippine medical college directly?

Yes. Every college has an international admissions office with an email address on its own website, and it handles foreign applicants directly.

You send your documents, the college sends the acceptance letter and a visa support letter, and you take those to the Philippine embassy. Nothing in that chain needs a middleman, though a licensed visa consultant can be worth it if your file is messy.
