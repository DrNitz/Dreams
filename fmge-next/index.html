After the MD

FMGE and NExT: the exam, the internship, the registration

A Philippine MD does not let you practise in India on its own. You pass the screening exam, do a one-year internship in India, then register with a state medical council. NExT is notified but deferred. Plan for the sequence, not the rumours.

Reviewed 27 November 2026. Rules and dates here come from NMC and NBEMS documents, linked at the foot of the page. Check the current bulletin before you act. CC BY 4.0 — reuse with attribution to drdreams.in.

Quick summary

A Philippine MD does not give an Indian graduate the right to practise in India on its own. You clear FMGE, or NExT when it is notified, complete a one-year compulsory rotating medical internship in India, and then get permanent registration from a state medical council. FMGE is conducted by NBEMS; the 2002 screening test regulations require 50 per cent in each paper, and the current attempt rules sit in the NBEMS bulletin. NExT was notified in the Gazette on 27 June 2023 and deferred by NMC on 13 July 2023 till further directions; its regulations describe a computer-based Step 1 of six papers and a clinical Step 2 of seven subjects, with both steps to be cleared within ten years of joining the MBBS course. NMC says it does not endorse any list of foreign medical colleges, and it listed 51,121 CRMI slots for foreign graduates for 2026–27 as on 31 October 2026. As of 2026, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Haryana do not require the Indian internship if the student completed it in the Philippines; this is subject to change, so confirm it in writing with the state medical council.

The part people skip

Passing FMGE does not make you a registered doctor in India. NMC's March 2026 notice says a state medical council cannot grant permanent registration until it has verified the exam, the clerkship and the one-year internship.

The order is fixed: exam, provisional registration, CRMI, permanent registration. Budget two to three years after graduation, and more if you need a second attempt.

The exams

FMGE and NExT, stated plainly

FMGE is a screening test for Indian citizens and Overseas Citizens of India who hold a primary medical qualification from outside India. It has been required for registration since 15 March 2002. It covers the Indian MBBS syllabus, which overlaps with what you learn in the Philippines but is not the same course. The differences are what catch people: Indian public health and forensic medicine, and the Indian legal and ethical framing of clinical questions.

The screening test regulations set three papers of multiple choice questions in pre-clinical, para-clinical and clinical subjects, in English, with 50 per cent required in each paper. Dates, the current syllabus and the attempt rules are announced by NBEMS, so read the bulletin for your year instead of an old regulation or a coaching post.

NExT is the National Exit Test. NMC notified the regulations on 27 June 2023 and then deferred the exam on 13 July 2023, till further directions from the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. The regulations describe two steps: a computer-based Step 1 with six papers in medicine, surgery, obstetrics and gynaecology, paediatrics, ENT and ophthalmology, and a clinical and viva Step 2 covering those six plus orthopaedics and physical medicine. Both steps have to be cleared within ten years of joining the MBBS course. NExT is meant to serve both as the licence exam and as the entrance for postgraduate seats. When it is in force, the corresponding older exam is phased out, but NMC can keep the old exam running for earlier batches.

What this means for your batch

You will sit whichever exam is in force when you graduate. As of this review, NExT stands deferred. That is not a reason to wait. The syllabus behind both exams is the same Indian MBBS syllabus, so start with the work, not the name.

The rules

What NMC actually says

NMC does not endorse a foreign college list. It says so on its own study-abroad page. The old MCI list is not a seal of approval. For screening test eligibility, the regulations accept a qualification named in the World Directory of Medical Schools or confirmed by the Indian embassy in that country as a recognised qualification. Check both. Get the fee structure, course content, duration and internship terms directly from the college in writing.

The NEET result is the eligibility certificate. For admissions from May 2018, the NEET-UG result stands in for the eligibility certificate. NMC says that result stays valid for three years from declaration and covers a pre-medical or language course followed by the MBBS. Keep the scorecard with the rest of the file.

The date you joined decides the rulebook. The Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate Regulations took effect on 18 November 2021. Join after that date and you are governed by the CRMI Regulations 2021, which require one year of internship in India. Join on or before that date and you can be assessed under the older screening test rules, provided the online classes were compensated physically and you did a year of internship at the foreign college.

The paperwork is checked, not assumed. NMC's 6 March 2026 notice says a compensation certificate must name the subjects and duration of theory, clinical work and clerkship. The transcript must be apostilled and authenticated by the Indian embassy. The state council verifies physical presence from visa stamps and passport entry and exit records.

A state council, not NMC, registers you. The March 2026 notice puts the job with the state medical council in consultation with the state directorate of medical education. The order is fixed: pass the exam, get provisional registration for CRMI, finish the year, then collect permanent registration. No council may grant permanent registration before those checks are complete. A few states currently waive the Indian year; see the internship section below.

The seats exist. NMC published 51,121 CRMI slots for foreign medical graduates for 2026–27 as on 31 October 2026. Its 2023 notice says the number of seats is 7.5 per cent of permitted MBBS seats in older colleges and 100 per cent in new colleges without their own interns. Allotment is handled by the state council and the DME together, and NMC says foreign graduates draw the same stipend as Indian interns at that institution. The delay comes from applications and verification, not from a shortage of notified seats.

Two deadlines to note. The December 2023 NMC notice says the internship must be completed within ten years of joining the foreign medical institution. NExT's regulations set the same ten-year window from MBBS admission for clearing both steps. NMC also published a draft FMGL amendment on 17 November 2026 proposing to change clause 4(b) of the 2021 regulations. A draft is not the final rule. Check the final gazette before you rely on it.

The plan

Five years, in slices

  1. Year one: one subject, one hour

    Pick anatomy and physiology. Watch Indian lectures for the topics your college is covering that week. You are not preparing for an exam yet. You are learning the Indian vocabulary for the same facts.

  2. Year two: add pathology and pharmacology

    Two to three hours a week, no more. Start a question bank and do ten questions a week. Keep a notebook of the ones you get wrong. That notebook becomes your revision list later.

  3. Year three: clinical subjects begin

    Now the ward and the exam point the same way. When you see a case, read the Indian guideline for it that evening. This is the year the two curricula stop feeling like separate courses.

  4. Year four: full question banks and past papers

    Timed weekly tests. Ten years of past papers at least. Join or form a group of three or four and teach each other, because teaching is the only way to find out what you do not actually know.

  5. Internship year: revision and mock exams

    Six to eight hours a day in the months before the exam, with mock tests on the same schedule as the real one. This is when the notebook from year two earns its keep.

Resources, without the brand war

Marrow, PrepLadder and DAMS are the Indian platforms students most often use, alongside past papers from NBEMS. DrDreams is not affiliated with any of them and is not saying one is better. Pick one and finish it. Collecting three subscriptions is not preparation.

What works

Habits that clear it

One resource, finished. Students who fail usually have four subscriptions and no completed course. Questions from the start, because reading without testing yourself feels productive and is not. A study group of three or four; ten becomes a social club. Timed mocks on the real schedule, because the exam is a stamina test as much as a knowledge test. Clinical work taken seriously, because the viva and the case discussion are where weak training shows.

What fails

Habits that sink people

"I will start after graduation." By then the basic sciences you need most are two years cold. Only watching videos: video feels like studying, recall is studying. Ignoring community medicine, which is a large, boring, high-scoring block. Studying alone for two years; isolation is a bigger risk than a noisy group. Planning around NExT rumours instead of studying the syllabus.

Coaching

Which colleges include it

Five of the six colleges covered here run FMGE coaching on campus: Gullas, Perpetual, DMSF, Brokenshire and Jose Maria, with the last two being smaller programmes. Southwestern does not. Check the current arrangement with the college before you assume either way.

On-campus coaching is worth more than it sounds, because it solves the discipline problem rather than the content problem. If your college does not have it, budget for an online course and treat the subscription as a fixed monthly cost, the same way you treat rent.

The order of events

Graduation is not the finish line. The route from a Philippine MD to an Indian white coat has four gates, and they happen in a fixed order:

  1. Graduate with the MD from a college that meets the foreign medical graduate requirements.
  2. Pass FMGE, or NExT when it is in force.
  3. Complete a twelve month compulsory rotating internship in India.
  4. Get permanent registration from a state medical council.

You cannot swap the order. You cannot do the Indian internship before the exam. You cannot register before the internship. And none of it fixes a college that did not meet the requirements when you joined. If you have not checked that yet, read is the degree valid in India first.

The twelve month Indian internship

After passing the exam, you get provisional registration and complete a twelve month compulsory rotating internship, usually called CRMI, at an approved institution in India. It rotates through medicine, surgery, obstetrics and gynaecology, paediatrics, community medicine and the allied departments.

Indian hospitals use this year to see whether your clinical training was hands-on. Students who spent five years watching from a corridor feel the difference in the first month. Students who clerked, presented and assisted in the Philippines settle in faster. NMC says foreign graduates at this stage receive the same stipend as Indian interns at that institution.

Apply early. Allotment runs through the state medical council and the state directorate of medical education, states have different processes, and the gap between passing the exam and starting the internship can be months. Ask both offices for the current process in writing.

State exception, as of 2026

Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Haryana do not currently require CRMI from a graduate who has completed the internship in the Philippines. Two conditions matter here. The internship in the Philippines must actually be done, with a certificate and logbook to show for it. And the position is as of 2026 only; state councils can change it. Get the current rule in writing from the state medical council before you plan around it, and keep the Philippine internship documents with your registration file.

Register with a state medical council

With the exam passed and the internship complete, you apply for permanent registration with the state medical council where you intend to practise. Registration is the legal step that makes you a doctor in India. It is separate from the degree, the WDOMS listing and the exam.

Keep the paper trail: degree, transcript, apostilled documents, the compensation certificate if you studied online, internship certificate, migration certificate, exam pass certificate, and the WDOMS and embassy records from your enrolment year. Registration offices can ask for documents years later, and a college that has changed administration may be slow to reissue them.

PG in India

If you want an MD or MS in India, the route is NEET-PG. Foreign graduates usually need to clear FMGE or NExT and complete the Indian internship before they are eligible, but the eligibility rules are set in the current NEET-PG information bulletin. Read it for your year, not a summary from a coaching centre. NExT's regulations say the exam will also be used for postgraduate entrance when it is in force.

PG preparation is a different exam from FMGE. FMGE tests the undergraduate syllabus; NEET-PG tests applied clinical decisions in more detail. Most students take a year or two of internship and preparation before sitting it. Starting early is an advantage, but burning out in first year is not.

Jobs after registration

Once registered, the ordinary options are:

  • Hospital medical officer. Private and charitable hospitals hire registered doctors for ward duty, emergency and outpatient work.
  • Primary care and clinics. General practice, family clinics and community health centres.
  • Government health schemes. State health departments and national programmes hire medical officers on contract and permanent roles.
  • Corporate and insurance medicine. Medical officers for companies, hospitals and health insurers, including claims and hospital review work.
  • Clinical research and medical writing. A less clinical path that values a medical degree and clear writing.
  • Hospital administration and public health. Usually after an additional degree or diploma.

None of these are guaranteed by the degree. They are the normal destinations for a registered Indian doctor, and the competition is the same as for any other graduate.

Options abroad

A Philippine MD is also a starting point for licensing routes elsewhere. Each has its own exam, fees and eligibility rules, and each should be checked at the official source for your year.

  • United States: USMLE steps and ECFMG certification. The college's WDOMS listing matters.
  • United Kingdom: PLAB and GMC registration.
  • Australia: AMC examinations and registration.
  • Canada: MCC examinations and provincial licensing.
  • Philippines: the Physician Licensure Examination and registration with the Professional Regulation Commission. The PLE, in detail.

These routes are expensive and slow, and they are not a fallback for a failed FMGE. Decide early if one of them is your plan, because the exam preparation starts years before graduation.

If you do not clear the exam

It happens, and it is not the end of your medical career. The first step is to check the current attempt rules in the NBEMS bulletin, because they decide what you can do next. The second is to diagnose why you failed: not enough clinical exposure, weak pre-clinical foundations, exam technique, or simply starting too late.

Then build a plan with a date. A repeat attempt with a fixed schedule, question bank targets and weekly tests is very different from "I will study harder". If you are working while preparing, protect two uninterrupted hours a day and one long test every week. The doctors who clear on a second attempt change the method, not just the hours.

Questions

After graduation, in plain answers

Is FMGE difficult for Philippines graduates?

It is difficult for everyone. Most candidates do not clear it on the first attempt. The ones who do usually started in the early years. The exam tests the Indian MBBS syllabus, which overlaps with yours but is not the same course.

Has NExT replaced FMGE yet?

No. NMC notified the National Exit Test regulations on 27 June 2023 and deferred the exam on 13 July 2023, till further directions from the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. Do not plan around a date from a coaching site. Check the current NMC notice before you act.

What happens after I clear FMGE?

You get provisional registration to do a one-year compulsory rotating medical internship in India. After you complete it, the state medical council grants permanent registration. NMC's March 2026 notice sets that order and says the council must verify the exam, the clerkship and the internship first.

Can I work in India right after the MD?

No. You need to pass FMGE or NExT, complete the twelve month Indian internship, and register with a state medical council. Only then can you practise.

Can I do PG in India after a Philippine MD?

Yes, through NEET-PG, usually after clearing FMGE or NExT and completing the Indian internship. Check the current NEET-PG information bulletin for eligibility. NExT's regulations say the exam will also be used for postgraduate entrance when it is in force.

What if I fail FMGE?

Check the attempt rules in the current NBEMS bulletin, diagnose why you failed, and build a fixed study plan with a date. Many doctors clear on a later attempt. It costs time and money, not your career.

Do I have to do the internship in India?

Yes, for Indian registration in most states. The Philippine internship is part of the MD. The Indian CRMI is a separate twelve month requirement after the exam. As of 2026, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Haryana do not require it if the internship was completed in the Philippines. State positions can change, so get confirmation in writing from your state medical council. Where CRMI applies, NMC's December 2023 notice says it must be completed within ten years of joining the foreign medical institution.

Can I practise in the Philippines instead?

Yes, since November 2026. The Philippine Professional Regulation Commission now permits Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools to sit the Physician Licensure Examination and register to practise in the country, which the Embassy of India in Manila confirmed. The PLE licences you in the Philippines only. To practise in India you still sit FMGE or NExT and finish the twelve month internship. The PLE, in detail.
