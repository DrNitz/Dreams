# The Philippine licensure exam, and how to get the licence

Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools can sit the Physician Licensure Examination since November 2026. This page covers the PRC route, who qualifies and what the licence does not give you.

**Quick summary.**

The Professional Regulation Commission opened the Physician Licensure Examination to Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools in November 2026, confirmed by the Embassy of India in Manila. Passing it registers you to practise in the Philippines as a general practitioner, and it can open the route to specialty training. It does not replace FMGE or NExT for practising in India; that is a separate exam and a separate 12-month internship. Draft rules allow direct registration without repeating the rotating internship in the Philippines, so confirm the current position with the regulator.

*Reviewed 25 November 2026. Based on the Embassy of India, Manila and the Philippine Professional Regulation Commission.*

## What changed

Philippine law lets a foreign medical graduate sit the PLE only if their own country gives Filipino doctors the same right to practise. India's position on that reciprocity was the block, and it kept the licence out of reach for Indian graduates.

In November 2026 the Philippine Professional Regulation Commission told the Embassy of India in Manila that Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools can sit the PLE and obtain registration to practise medicine in the country. The Ambassador, Harsh Kumar Jain, announced it. The change also matters for India, because NMC's rules look at whether you have the right to practise in the country where you took the degree.

*The sequence*

## How the licence is obtained

Five steps, in order. The PRC publishes the exact document list and the exam dates for each cycle, so treat the detail here as the shape of the process and the PRC page as the checklist.

1. ### Finish a CHED-recognised Doctor of Medicine

   The degree has to come from a Philippine medical school that CHED recognises, and it has to include the twelve-month internship. Your college's registrar issues the transcript and the certificate of graduation or registrability that the PRC asks for.
2. ### Apply to the PRC for the examination

   The Professional Regulation Commission runs the application, not the college. You submit the PRC form with the documents it lists for the cycle: the transcript, the diploma or certificate, proof of the internship, clearances and photographs. The PRC announces the window, and late files wait for the next one.
3. ### Sit the Physician Licensure Examination

   The PLE is the written examination set by the PRC's Board of Medicine. It is held on scheduled dates, and the PRC publishes the coverage and the testing centres with the announcement. You sit it as a foreign graduate under the reciprocity route the PRC opened in November 2026.
4. ### Take the oath and register

   Passing the exam is not the licence. You take the physician's oath, pay the registration fee and receive the certificate of registration and the professional identification card. The PRC controls that step, and the dates are on its announcements.
5. ### Keep the registration current

   The PRC licence is renewable, and practising without a current one is a problem in any country. If you stay in the Philippines, the renewal dates matter the same way your visa dates do. If you come home, the licence stays a Philippine credential and does not replace an Indian registration.

**What this page can and cannot tell you.**

The PRC has not published a standing list of documents for Indian applicants on this route. The sequence above is the process the PRC describes for foreign graduates, and the documents are the ones it lists for the cycle. Confirm the current list with the PRC and with your college's international office before you pay an application fee.

*The record*

## How the position moved, in order

Each step was a real document or decision, not a rumour. The June facts are in the Embassy's advisory on its own site.

1. ### CHED's letter, 8 May 2025

   The Philippine Commission on Higher Education told the Embassy that the Doctor of Medicine meets the Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate requirements: 54 months, the prescribed subjects, English, a twelve month internship, and a certificate of registrability.
2. ### House Resolution No. 73, 11 March 2026

   The House of Representatives of the Philippines endorsed that the MD from CHED-recognised institutions complies with the NMC regulations for Indian students studying abroad.
3. ### Embassy advisory, 17 June 2026

   The Embassy of India, Manila set out the position in writing, and noted that the reciprocity guidance was still awaited. This is the page to read on the Embassy's own site.
4. ### Embassy letter to the Philippine DFA, 8 October 2026

   The Embassy asked the Philippine Department of Foreign Affairs for reciprocal recognition, pointing to the registration rights Filipino doctors hold in India.
5. ### PRC decision, November 2026

   The Professional Regulation Commission confirmed that Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools can sit the PLE and register to practise. This is the step that ends the wait.

*The boundary*

## What it does not do

Passing the PLE licences you in the Philippines. It does not register you in India. To practise in India you still sit FMGE or NExT and complete the twelve month Indian internship, whichever country licensed you first. The two are separate routes, and the PLE does not shorten the Indian one.

If your plan is to come home and work in India, nothing about this changes your timeline. The [FMGE and NExT page](../fmge-next/) is the one you want. The PLE matters if you want the option to stay, work and be registered in the Philippines, or if you want your file to satisfy every NMC condition while you are there.

*Questions*

## Two questions, answered

**Can Indian graduates take the Philippine PLE now?**

Yes, since November 2026. The Philippine Professional Regulation Commission permits Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools to sit the Physician Licensure Examination and register to practise in the country. The Embassy of India in Manila confirmed the change. Before this, Philippine law required India to give Filipino doctors the same right, and it did not, so the route was closed.

**Does passing the PLE let me practise in India?**

No. To practise in India you still sit FMGE or NExT and complete the twelve month Indian internship, whatever licence you hold in the Philippines. The PLE opens the Philippines route only.
