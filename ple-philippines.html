Licensure

The Philippine licensure exam, and what changed in November

For years the Philippine Physician Licensure Examination was shut to Indian graduates, because the law required India to give Filipino doctors the same right. In November 2026 the Philippine Professional Regulation Commission opened it. Here is what that means, and what it does not.

Reviewed November 2026. Based on the Embassy of India, Manila and the Philippine Professional Regulation Commission.

The facts

Exam
Physician Licensure Examination (PLE)
Regulator
Professional Regulation Commission (PRC)
Who can sit it
Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools
What it gives you
Registration to practise in the Philippines
What it does not
Registration in India (that is FMGE or NExT)
Confirmed by
Embassy of India, Manila

What changed

Philippine law lets a foreign medical graduate sit the PLE only if their own country gives Filipino doctors the same right to practise. India's position on that reciprocity was the block, and it kept the licence out of reach for Indian graduates.

In November 2026 the Philippine Professional Regulation Commission told the Embassy of India in Manila that Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools can sit the PLE and obtain registration to practise medicine in the country. The Ambassador, Harsh Kumar Jain, announced it. The change also matters for India, because NMC's rules look at whether you have the right to practise in the country where you took the degree.

The record

How the position moved, in order

Each step was a real document or decision, not a rumour. The June facts are in the Embassy's advisory on its own site.

  1. CHED's letter, 8 May 2025

    The Philippine Commission on Higher Education told the Embassy that the Doctor of Medicine meets the Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate requirements: 54 months, the prescribed subjects, English, a twelve month internship, and a certificate of registrability.

  2. House Resolution No. 73, 11 March 2026

    The House of Representatives of the Philippines endorsed that the MD from CHED-recognised institutions complies with the NMC regulations for Indian students studying abroad.

  3. Embassy advisory, 17 June 2026

    The Embassy of India, Manila set out the position in writing, and noted that the reciprocity guidance was still awaited. This is the page to read on the Embassy's own site.

  4. Embassy letter to the Philippine DFA, 8 October 2026

    The Embassy asked the Philippine Department of Foreign Affairs for reciprocal recognition, pointing to the registration rights Filipino doctors hold in India.

  5. PRC decision, November 2026

    The Professional Regulation Commission confirmed that Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools can sit the PLE and register to practise. This is the step that ends the wait.

The boundary

What it does not do

Passing the PLE licences you in the Philippines. It does not register you in India. To practise in India you still sit FMGE or NExT and complete the twelve month Indian internship, whichever country licensed you first. The two are separate routes, and the PLE does not shorten the Indian one.

If your plan is to come home and work in India, nothing about this changes your timeline. The FMGE and NExT page is the one you want. The PLE matters if you want the option to stay, work and be registered in the Philippines, or if you want your file to satisfy every NMC condition while you are there.

Before you rely on it

What to check yourself

The rules are fresh, and a page like this one goes stale. Confirm the current position with the two authorities before you build a plan on it.

  • The Professional Regulation Commission, at prc.gov.ph, for the PLE schedule, the application requirements and the current eligibility rules.
  • The Embassy of India, Manila, for the advisory text and any later update: eoimanila.gov.in/edphilli.
  • Your college's international office, for how its own graduates have applied and what it will put in writing.

This page states the position as reported in November 2026. If a newer notice changes it, write in and the page is corrected, with the date changed.

Questions

Two questions, answered

Can Indian graduates take the Philippine PLE now?

Yes, since November 2026. The Philippine Professional Regulation Commission permits Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools to sit the Physician Licensure Examination and register to practise in the country. The Embassy of India in Manila confirmed the change. Before this, Philippine law required India to give Filipino doctors the same right, and it did not, so the route was closed.

Does passing the PLE let me practise in India?

No. To practise in India you still sit FMGE or NExT and complete the twelve month Indian internship, whatever licence you hold in the Philippines. The PLE opens the Philippines route only.
