# MBBS in the Philippines fees: what five years really cost

₹28 to ₹35 lakh for the whole MD, of which ₹20 to ₹24 lakh is college fees. The rest is hostel, food, flights, visa renewals and exam preparation, line by line.

**Quick summary.**

The full cost of a Philippine MD runs ₹28 to ₹35 lakh over five and a half to six years. College fees are ₹20 to ₹24 lakh of that; hostel, food, return flights, visa renewals, insurance and exam preparation make up the rest. Published 2026-2027 fee sheets exist for Gullas, DMSF, Jose Maria and Brokenshire, and this page reproduces them with the exchange rate and the date. Southwestern and Perpetual quote on request. Never pay the full course fee upfront to an agency: pay in instalments, and pay MD tuition directly to the college at official rates. The cheapest first year is not the cheapest degree, so add the hidden lines before you compare.

**Why the published sheets live on the college pages.**

Four of the six colleges have published 2026-2027 fee sheets, and they are on each college page and summarised below. Sheets change every intake, sometimes twice a year, so treat them as a starting point and confirm the current sheet in writing before you pay.

What this page can do is tell you exactly which lines to ask for, and how they add up. That is worth more than a number that ages badly.

*The lines*

## Eight things you will pay for

Ask for each of these separately, in writing, from the college. A quote that cannot be broken into these lines is either disorganised or hiding something.

1. ### Tuition, per year

   The biggest line, and the one that rises. Ask for the fee for each year of the programme, not just year one. Some colleges hold it flat, some do not.
2. ### Hostel

   Per month or per semester. Confirm whether air conditioning, electricity and water are included. In Cebu and Davao, a basic hostel is cheap; a comfortable one is not.
3. ### Food

   An Indian mess with a cook who understands your masala is the cheapest option where one exists. Where it does not, you cook on an induction plate with turmeric, cumin, coriander and chilli powder carried in checked luggage, because those spices cost two or three times more in Cebu. Rice and egg will carry you through the first month. This line is where the cheapest college turns expensive.
4. ### Flights, twice a year

   Manila and Cebu are easier and cheaper to reach than Davao. Philippine immigration expects a real round-trip ticket on entry; a one-way ticket with an agency dummy return is not the same thing. Book the return. Multiply by five years. It is a real line, not a rounding error.
5. ### Visa and immigration costs

   The 9(F) visa conversion, then annual renewals and registration with Philippine immigration. Agencies handle the paperwork because it is tedious, not because it is secret. These charges recur every year and are almost never in the quote you get in India. On the 9A-to-9F route after arrival, conversion is ₱14,500 and yearly extension ₱16,000. Add the ACR I-Card at ₱2,500 to ₱4,500 plus the card fee, medical at ₱1,500 to ₱4,000 and NBI clearance at ₱150 to ₱200. Agencies ask ₱20,000 to ₱30,000 for the same file. The [9F conversion page](../9f-visa-conversion/) has the steps and requirements. The [Bureau of Immigration page](https://immigration.gov.ph/student-visa-9f/) also posts its own amounts (₱9,720 conversion, ₱4,020 extension), but that table is dated 2014. The figures here are the 2026-27 amounts people actually pay.
6. ### Health insurance

   Usually required, usually annual, and often forgotten until enrolment week.
7. ### FMGE coaching

   Included at some colleges, separate at others, and not offered at all at one. If it is separate, price an online Indian course before you choose, because you will need one.
8. ### A buffer

   One emergency flight home, one medical bill, one laptop replacement. Every batch has a student who needed it. Put it in the budget from the start.

Hostel and food is the biggest variable in the total. The packaged rate on those sheets is ₱14,000 to ₱15,000 a month. Sharing a room and cooking brings it to ₱8,000 to ₱10,000, and over sixty months that difference is a few lakh rupees, which is why two students at the same college can spend very different amounts.

## Against the other destinations

Indicative ranges students report, not quotes. Treat them as a starting point for your own calls.

Indicative ranges and the practical differences, not a price list.

| Destination | Typically quoted | Language of teaching | The real difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Philippines** | ₹28 to ₹35 lakh | English | Clinical access for foreign students, tropical climate, 5 to 6 hour flight |
| Russia | ₹20 to ₹35 lakh | Russian in the early years | Cold winters, limited patient contact for foreign students |
| Georgia | ₹25 to ₹40 lakh | English, heavy accent | High cost, small Indian community, cold winters |
| China | ₹20 to ₹30 lakh | Mandarin by the clinical years | Language barrier in hospitals, very different food culture |
| Kazakhstan | ₹18 to ₹25 lakh | Russian and Kazakh | Fewer Indian students, little FMGE coaching infrastructure |
| Ukraine | Not recommended now | Ukrainian and Russian | Ongoing conflict. Students have had to leave mid-course |

The point of the table is not that the Philippines wins. It is that the cheapest headline is rarely the cheapest degree, and the language of instruction changes your entire clinical training. A destination where you cannot speak to a patient is not cheaper, it is a different course.

*Worth paying for*

## Where money is well spent

- **A visa consultant, if the file is messy.** One rejection costs a semester. A licensed consultant who has processed Philippine student visas before earns the fee in that one case.
- **A good hostel in year one.** Security and a short commute matter more than saving ₱2,000 a month.
- **An FMGE course from first year.** The cheapest insurance you will ever buy against repeating an exam.
- **Health insurance.** Non-negotiable. One hospital admission without it can cost more than a year of premiums.

*Not worth paying for*

## Where money disappears

- **Cash paid without a receipt.** Fees go to the institution and come back on college letterhead. If the receipt is a handwritten note, it is not a receipt.
- **"Documentation charges" on top of a quote.** Photocopies and couriers cost a few hundred rupees. Anything larger needs an itemised bill.
- **Deposits paid before an acceptance letter exists.** The letter is the only thing that proves a seat. Pay after it, not before it.
- **Two quotes that do not match.** Ask both for the same eight lines. The gap between them is the part nobody explained.

**Before any transfer.**

No college in the Philippines needs money sent to an individual's personal account. Fees go to the institution, and you get a receipt on college letterhead. If the account name is a person, stop and ask why in writing.

Never pay the full course fee upfront to an agency. Agencies bundle the first-year BSBio pre-med course with documentation, Delhi attestation, PCC, visa processing, flight and uniform, and take a markup on that bundle. From the MD years onward, tuition goes directly to the college at its official rates. Pay in installments, and keep every receipt.

*Questions*

## The money questions, answered plainly

**What is the total cost of MBBS in the Philippines?**

Most families spend between roughly ₹28 lakh and ₹35 lakh across the whole programme. College fees are ₹20 lakh to ₹24 lakh of that, and hostel, food, flights and visa renewals make up the rest. The spread comes from the college, the city and whether the student cooks. DMSF sits above it, around ₹38 lakh, because its tuition is quoted in dollars.

Ask each college for a current fee sheet rather than relying on any published figure, including this one.

**Is it cheaper than a private medical college in India?**

The tuition is lower than a private Indian medical seat, which is often quoted in tens of lakhs before any donation. It is not cheap in absolute terms, and the living costs of five and a half years abroad are real. Compare the all-in total, not the first-year fee.

**What costs get left out of a fee quote?**

Visa renewals, the annual registration with Philippine immigration, health insurance, hostel deposits, local fees payable in ₱, flights home, and FMGE coaching. Those six lines are where a quote that looked affordable turns into a budget problem in year two.

## Ask for the fee sheet

*Every college has a fee sheet for the intake. Ask for it in writing, with hostel and miscellaneous lines included. If someone quotes a single total instead, ask which lines it covers.*
