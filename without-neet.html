MBBS in the Philippines without NEET

There is a route. It does not skip the exam. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you a problem you will meet again in year five, in a room in India, with an NMC officer asking for your NEET result.

Reviewed November 2026. Rules change, so check the current NMC position before you commit money.

Read this first

NMC requires NEET for Indian students who want to practise in India. That requirement sits on the student, not on the college. A Philippine college can admit you to a pre-medical course without NEET. It cannot make you eligible for Indian registration without it.

So the honest framing is this: you are not choosing between NEET and no NEET. You are choosing where and when you sit it.

The route

What BSBio actually is

BSBio is Bachelor of Science in Biology, a normal undergraduate science degree in the Philippines. It is a pre-medical course, six months to a year of it depending on how the college structures the transfer, and it exists in Philippine universities independently of any Indian student. It is not a medical degree, and it is not a back door.

The arrangement Indian students use is straightforward. You enrol in BSBio, which does not require NEET. While you study, you prepare for and sit NEET. When you clear it, the same university moves you into the MD programme. You have spent the year in an English-medium science classroom, in the city you are going to live in, with the paperwork for your student visa already done.

What it does not do is reduce the MD by a year. The MD is still about five and a half years. Add the BSBio year and you are looking at six to six and a half years abroad, plus the FMGE and the Indian internship afterwards.

Check the bachelor's degree itself

The Philippine Doctor of Medicine is a postgraduate course, and enrolment needs a bachelor's degree and the NMAT. The Embassy of India, Manila is clear that the pre-medical degree must itself be accredited by CHED, that its content must meet the MD eligibility requirements, and that its duration must follow CHED's guidelines. Ask the college to put all three in writing before you enrol in BSBio.

BSBio compared with the other pre-med courses

Philippine universities run several pre-medical degrees, and Indian students ask which one is best. The honest answer is that only two routes matter for you: the BSBio bridging year, or direct entry to the MD after class 12. The four-year degrees below are for Filipino students who are still deciding on a career.

One thing none of these do is shorten the medical course. NMC counts the 54 months of the MD and the twelve-month internship, not the year before it. A four-year pre-med degree does not buy you a shorter MD, and it does not exempt you from FMGE.

Who the BSBio year genuinely helps

  • Students who missed NEET by a margin they can close. A year of structured biology in English, with fewer distractions, is a real second attempt with better conditions.
  • Students who need to be somewhere else to focus. Some people cannot study at home. It helps to admit that, and it says nothing bad about you.
  • Families who want the landing spread out. Arriving a year before the MD starts means the culture shock, the cooking, the accent and the homesickness happen outside the hardest academic year.

Who it does not help

  • Students who did not study at all. A new country does not fix a study habit. It removes the support that was hiding the problem.
  • Anyone told they can skip NEET entirely. They cannot, and whoever told them knows it.
  • Anyone on a tight budget. The extra year costs an extra year: tuition, hostel, food, flights, visa renewal. It is the single most expensive way to prepare for an exam you could have prepared for at home.

Step by step

The sequence, and where it goes wrong

  1. Confirm the college offers BSBio and a transfer

    Not every college on this site runs the route. Ask for the transfer arrangement in writing: the conditions, the timeline, and whether a seat in the MD is held or merely hoped for.

  2. Apply to BSBio

    12th marksheet, passport, birth certificate, photographs. No NEET result is required for this step. The document list is the same one you will use later.

  3. Get the acceptance letter and the visa

    The letter names the course. Keep it. You will need it again at the embassy, at immigration, and at enrolment.

  4. Study BSBio and prepare for NEET in parallel

    This is the year. Two to three hours a day of NEET preparation on top of classes is the realistic load. The biology overlaps, which helps, but the chemistry and physics do not teach themselves.

  5. Sit NEET and clear it

    Register as a private candidate, travel if you must, and sit the exam. This is the step that decides whether the year was a detour or a dead end.

  6. Transfer into the MD

    Get the transfer in writing, with the date. Do not rely on a verbal assurance from an admissions clerk three months before the semester starts.

Where it goes wrong

The common failure is not the exam. It is the transfer. Students arrive on a BSBio acceptance letter with a verbal promise of an MD seat, then discover the MD intake is full, or the credits do not transfer the way they were told, or the college has changed the arrangement. Get every one of those in writing before you pay.

The alternative

If you already did a year of NEET coaching

Students who have already spent a year in serious NEET coaching usually do not need BSBio. If your score qualifies you, you go straight into the MD. The coaching year already did the job BSBio would do, and you save a year of fees and a year of your life.

Talk to the college about its current entry requirements for the MD directly. Requirements have changed before and will change again, and the only version that counts is the one the college confirms in writing for your intake. The NMC rules page tracks the regulatory side, with the official notice and PDF.

What it costs, roughly

A BSBio year is cheaper than an MD year, but it is not free: tuition, hostel, food, flights and a visa renewal. Add it to your five-year total before you decide. The cost page has a worksheet for exactly this.

The straight answer

If you can clear NEET at home with one more year of preparation, that is cheaper and faster than doing it in Cebu. The BSBio route makes sense when the year abroad is genuinely better for you than the year at home, not when it is a way to avoid the exam.

Questions

What students ask about this route

Can I study MBBS in the Philippines without NEET?

You can enrol in the BSBio pre-medical year without NEET at colleges that offer it. You cannot qualify to practise in India without clearing NEET, because NMC requires it for Indian students. The BSBio year buys preparation time, not an exemption.

How long does the BSBio route add?

Six months to a year, depending on the college and when you clear NEET and transfer. The MD itself remains about five and a half years including internship.

Is the BSBio route legal and safe?

BSBio is a normal Philippine pre-medical degree and many Indian students take it. The risk is not the course, it is the promise around it. Confirm with the college in writing that your BSBio year transitions into their MD, and confirm NMC's current requirements before you pay.

The exam is still the plan

The biology year buys time. It does not buy a licence, and NEET still has to be cleared. The FMGE page explains the exam that comes after the degree.
