The five clarifications

Read these in order. The first one decides which rulebook applies to you, and you do not get to choose.

1. Which rulebook applies, decided by your admission date

If you joined your foreign course before 18 November 2021, you are governed by the Screening Test Regulations 2002, provided you compensated your online study with physical onsite clinical or theory training, completed a year of internship at the foreign university, and passed examinations equivalent to the Indian MBBS.

If you joined on or after 18 November 2021, the FMGL Regulations 2021 and the CRMI Regulations 2021 apply. That means a one-year compulsory rotating internship in India after your foreign degree. The conditions in the 7 December 2023 notice remain in force.

"The students who had taken admission on or before 18.11.2021, the date of notification of the Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate Regulations, 2021, and who have duly compensated the period of online instruction by undergoing physical onsite clinical and/or theory training, together with one year of internship in the concerned foreign medical college or university, and who have qualified the examinations equivalent to the MBBS examination in India, shall be considered in accordance with the provisions of the Screening Test Regulations."

Source: NMC Public Notice U-15024/15/2024-UGMEB(Pt), 5 March 2026

2. Online study cannot be compensated without extending the course

This is the sentence that invalidates a lot of certificates already sitting in students' files. Compensation without an extension of the study period is not accepted. The course and clerkship must be comparable with the Indian MBBS under the Graduate Medical Education Regulations 1997, and medical training needs substantial hands-on clinical work that virtual instruction cannot replace.

If any part of your course was online, you need equivalent in-person training for that exact duration.

"Compensation without extension of the study period is not acceptable, as it is contrary to FMGL Regulations, 2021, which require the course and clerkship to be commensurate with the MBBS course in India... foreign medical institutions that issue a 'compensation certificate' without actually extending the period of study and without ensuring that the additional training has occurred in a physical mode are acting in contradiction to the intent and requirements of the FMGL Regulations. Such certificates, issued without genuine compensation and extension of study period, undermine the very spirit and purpose of the FMGL Regulations, 2021."

Source: NMC Public Notice U-15024/15/2024-UGMEB(Pt), 5 March 2026

3. What a valid compensation certificate must contain

The certificate has to specify the subjects completed during offline compensation, both theory and practical; the clinical subjects undertaken onsite; the mandatory clerkship completed during the compensation period; and the extended academic period, meaning the exact duration added. The transcript of record must also be apostilled and authenticated by the Indian embassy or high commission in that country.

"It is imperative to verify the authenticity of the claim of Foreign Medical Graduates (FMGs) regarding the offline compensation for the remaining duration of the medical course, which must include both theoretical and practical components. Accordingly, the compensation certificate issued by the concerned foreign medical institution must clearly specify the details of the subjects and the duration completed by the FMG, i.e., The certificate must specify theory, clinical subjects, and mandatory clerkship undertaken during offline compensation, etc."

Source: NMC Public Notice U-15024/15/2024-UGMEB(Pt), 5 March 2026

4. Two academic years cannot be compressed into one

The notice answers this directly, because students were trying it. Academic years cannot be compressed, and the whole online period must be compensated with physical onsite training. You cannot finish a two-year curriculum in one year of offline study.

"Whether the curriculum for the two academic years can be completed in one year of offline study? No. As clarified at Sl. No. 1, academic years cannot be compressed, and the entire online study period must be adequately compensated through physical onsite training."

Source: NMC Public Notice U-15024/15/2024-UGMEB(Pt), 5 March 2026

5. State Medical Councils carry the verification

Primary responsibility for processing foreign medical graduate applications sits with the State Medical Councils, in consultation with the State Directorates of Medical Education. They must confirm that the compensatory academics happened at the same foreign institution, verify visa stamps and entry and exit records, confirm the screening test, and confirm the one-year CRMI in India.

The sequence is: qualify the screening test and submit the degree and clerkship certificates, get provisional registration for CRMI, then get permanent registration after completing it. No council may grant permanent registration without full verification, and a registration granted outside these rules can be revoked.

"The primary responsibility for processing applications of Foreign Medical Graduates (FMGs)... rests exclusively with the respective State Medical Councils (SMCs), in consultation with the concerned State Directorate of Medical Education (DME)."

Source: NMC Public Notice U-15024/15/2024-UGMEB(Pt), 5 March 2026
If you studied in the Philippines between 2020 and 2022

Contact your university now and ask for a compensation certificate that states the extended period. Get the transcript apostilled and authenticated at the Indian embassy in Manila. Do not wait until graduation, when the registrar has less reason to help you and you have less time to fix it.

Timeline, 2002 to 2026

Every rule that changes what your degree is worth in India, in the order it happened.

  • 2002: Screening Test Regulations

    MCI introduces FMGE. Every foreign medical graduate must pass it to practise in India. No NEET requirement yet.

  • 2018: NEET becomes mandatory for studying abroad

    An amendment makes NEET compulsory for Indian students seeking admission to a foreign medical college.

  • 18 November 2021: FMGL Regulations

    NMC replaces MCI. A 54-month minimum course, a 12-month internship at the same foreign university, WDOMS listing, NEET and English medium instruction all become conditions.

  • 18 November 2021: CRMI Regulations

    Compulsory rotating internship in India for every post-2021 foreign graduate, with a 7.5 per cent seat cap in older colleges.

  • 7 December 2023: public notice

    Ukraine relaxations, recognition of the BS course for pre-2021 students, the 7.5 per cent cap, the 10-year limit, and eligibility certificate rules.

  • 19 June 2024: public notice

    Students who genuinely compensated online teaching with onsite training remain eligible for CRMI.

  • 8 May 2025: CHED letter

    The Philippine Commission on Higher Education tells the Indian Embassy that the Doctor of Medicine meets the FMGL conditions: 54 months, the prescribed subjects, English, a twelve month internship, and a certificate of registrability.

  • 5 March 2026: NMC public notice

    Compensation must extend the course, certificates without an extension are invalid, academic years cannot be compressed, and State Medical Councils carry verification. The PDF is at the top of this page.

  • 11 March 2026: House Resolution No. 73

    The House of Representatives of the Philippines endorses that the MD from CHED-recognised institutions complies with the NMC regulations for Indian students abroad.

  • 17 June 2026: Embassy advisory

    The Embassy of India, Manila sets out the position in writing, with the CHED letter and the Resolution. The PLE page has the sequence.

  • November 2026: the PLE opens

    The Philippine Professional Regulation Commission tells the Embassy that Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools can sit the Physician Licensure Examination and register to practise. What that means.

FMGL 2021: seven rules that cannot be worked around

Break any one of these and the degree does not convert into an Indian registration. The 2026 notice adds detail to them, it does not relax them.

  1. 54 months of academic study

    At least four and a half years, excluding internship, comparable with the Indian MBBS curriculum. Older four-year programmes no longer qualify.

  2. 12-month internship at the same institution

    Not at another hospital, and not in India. Same university as the academic course.

  3. NEET before admission

    Qualify NEET-UG before joining the foreign medical degree. BSBio sits before the degree, which is why it is treated differently.

  4. English medium

    The whole course in English, with an English medium certificate from the university.

  5. WDOMS listing

    The university must appear in the World Directory of Medical Schools. Check search.wdoms.org before paying anything.

  6. Same institution for study and internship

    Transfers between universities are generally not permitted, outside specific NMC-approved schemes.

  7. Eligibility certificate

    Students joining after 2021 should obtain an NMC eligibility certificate before joining the course.

FMGE eligibility, in five conditions

  1. A primary medical qualification from outside India

    An MD or MBBS equivalent awarded by a foreign institution.

  2. WDOMS listing at the time of admission

    Not later, and not "in process".

  3. NEET qualified before joining

    For admissions after 2018.

  4. 54 months of study and 12 months of internship

    Both at the same institution, with the internship onsite.

  5. Valid compensation, if any study was online

    With the extended period stated, as the March 2026 notice requires.

Questions

What people ask about these rules

What is the new NMC notification of March 2026 about?

It clarifies that online study cannot be compensated without genuinely extending the study period, sets out what a valid compensation certificate must contain, confirms that academic years cannot be compressed, and places verification responsibility on State Medical Councils.

Where can I download the March 2026 notification?

The official PDF of notification U-15024/15/2024-UGMEB(Pt) is linked at the top of this page. Always use the regulator's copy rather than a screenshot or a forwarded image.

Is there a ban on Philippines MBBS in 2026?

There is no ban. There is tighter verification. The degree is treated as valid only if the college is listed with NMC and WDOMS, the course meets the 54-month requirement, and the internship was completed onsite at the same institution.

The six colleges covered here are checked against both lists. Verify them yourself anyway.

What is the 54-month rule?

Under the FMGL Regulations 2021 the foreign medical course must run at least 54 months of academic study, excluding internship, and must be comparable with the Indian MBBS curriculum. Older four-year programmes no longer qualify.

What must a compensation certificate contain?

The subjects completed during offline compensation, both theory and practical. The clinical subjects undertaken onsite. The mandatory clerkship. And the extended academic period, meaning the exact duration added. Certificates without a genuine extension are invalid.

I joined before 18 November 2021. Which rules apply?

The Screening Test Regulations 2002, provided you compensated online study with physical onsite training and completed a year of internship at the foreign university. Students who joined later fall under FMGL 2021 and CRMI 2021, including the one-year internship in India.

Is the Philippines MD valid in India in 2026?

Yes, if the college is NMC-listed and WDOMS-listed, the course meets 54 months, and the internship was onsite at the same institution. After that you clear FMGE and complete the 12-month CRMI in India. Verify the college on both lists before admission, not after.

What is the 7.5 per cent CRMI seat cap?

Older Indian medical colleges may allocate up to 7.5 per cent of intern seats to foreign medical graduates. New colleges without their own interns can offer more. In practice this creates waiting periods, which is another reason to clear FMGE early.

Can I do my internship in India instead of the Philippines?

No. The 12-month internship must be completed at the same foreign institution where you studied. The 12-month CRMI in India comes after that, so it is a second internship, not a replacement.

What if my college is not on WDOMS?

The degree will not support an Indian registration. WDOMS is the first filter NMC uses, and the 2026 notice makes State Medical Councils verify against it. Check before you pay fees.

Do I need NEET before joining BSBio?

BSBio is a pre-medical course rather than the medical degree, so the NEET requirement attaches to the MD admission that follows. Confirm the current position with NMC and the college before you pay for either. The BSBio route explained.

Will NExT replace FMGE?

NExT is intended to replace FMGE, and the timeline has been deferred more than once. FMGE remains the active test, so prepare for the syllabus rather than the name. A study plan by year.

What is the 10-year limit?

The course and internship must be completed within 10 years of joining the foreign institution, including gaps and repeated years. The December 2023 notice states that graduates who exceed it lose eligibility for registration in India.

Do these rules apply to students who started in 2026?

If you enrol now, you are firmly under FMGL 2021, CRMI 2021 and the 2026 clarifications. That means 54 months, onsite internship at the same institution, NEET before the MD, WDOMS listing, and a 12-month internship in India afterwards.

The main guide FMGE and NExT

The short answer

An MD from a Philippine medical college is valid in India if three things are true: the college was on the National Medical Commission's list when you enrolled and when you graduated, you pass FMGE or NExT, and you complete a twelve month internship in India. Miss any one of those and the degree will not get you registered to practise.

"Valid" is not a property of the country. It is a property of the college, the year you joined, and what you do after you graduate. Two students can hold the same degree from the same university and have very different outcomes because one checked the list and the other trusted a brochure.

This page is the long version of that answer. The NMC rules page has the official notice and the exact clauses.

What "valid" actually means

People use "recognised" to mean three separate things, and that is where the confusion starts.

  • Recognition. The college appears on the NMC's list of foreign medical institutions. This is about the college, not you.
  • Equivalence. NMC treats the Philippine Doctor of Medicine as equivalent to the Indian MBBS for registration. This is about the degree.
  • Registration. You pass the screening exam, finish the Indian internship, and your state medical council enters you in the register. This is about you.

All three have to line up. A college on the list does not register you. A passed exam does not fix a college that was never listed. And a degree from a listed college does not practise for you while you skip the internship.

Check one: the college on the NMC list

Go to nmc.org.in and find the list of recognised foreign medical institutions. Search the college by its official name, not the short name in a WhatsApp forward. Names differ: a college may be listed under its university, its trust, or an older name.

Then check the same college on the World Directory of Medical Schools at search.wdoms.org. NMC's 2026 notice leans on WDOMS for verification, and a college that is absent there is a problem even if a consultant says otherwise.

Two practical notes. First, the list changes. A college can be added or removed, and the rules put the burden on the student to confirm status at enrolment and at graduation. Second, a screenshot is not proof. Take your own screenshot with the date visible, and ask the college for a written statement that it is listed for the year you are joining.

Check two: WDOMS

WDOMS is not an approval body. It is a directory, run by the World Federation for Medical Education and FAIMER. Being in it does not by itself make a degree valid in India, and being absent from it is a red flag that NMC now treats seriously.

Use it for the details that brochures blur: the exact degree awarded, the address, the year the programme started, and whether the school is listed as a medical school or only as a health science institution. If the WDOMS entry and the brochure disagree about the degree name, ask the college to explain in writing.

The 2026 NMC rules

The NMC public notice of 5 March 2026 tightened the route for foreign graduates. The parts that matter before you join:

  • The course must run at least 54 months. A shorter programme, or one that compresses teaching into fewer months, creates a registration problem later.
  • The internship must be at the same university. The twelve month internship has to be done at the institution that awarded the degree, not at a separate hospital arranged at the end.
  • Online teaching must be compensated. If part of your course was delivered online, the college has to make up the lost time. Do not enrol in a programme that plans to teach a large share of the pre-clinical years remotely.
  • The whole programme must be completed within ten years. Breaks, repeated years and deferred internships count against that window.
  • FMGL 2021 applies to students who enrolled on or after 18 November 2021. If you joined after that date, the 2021 regulations govern you, not the older framework.

These are the rules as published. They can be amended, and state medical councils sometimes read them differently. Read the full notice page and keep the PDF. If a consultant tells you a rule does not apply to your college, ask them to show you the clause.

FMGE or NExT

After the MD you sit the Foreign Medical Graduate Examination, or its successor, the National Exit Test. FMGE is conducted by the National Board of Examinations in Medical Sciences. NExT is being introduced and is expected to replace FMGE over time. Until the transition is complete, check the current NBEMS information bulletin for which exam applies to your batch, the dates, the eligibility rules and the attempt rules.

The exam is not a formality. It tests the Indian clinical and pre-clinical syllabus, and the pass rate for foreign graduates has historically been low. The students who clear it usually started preparing in first year, not after the internship. The FMGE and NExT page has a year-by-year plan.

The twelve month internship in India

Passing the exam is not the end. You then complete a twelve month compulsory rotating internship, usually called CRMI, at an approved institution in India. The internship is supervised, rotating through the major departments, and it is the point where Indian hospitals see whether your clinical training was real.

Plan the internship early. Seats are limited, the application windows are fixed, and a gap between passing the exam and starting the internship costs you months. Ask your state medical council and the institution you are targeting for the current process, not a forum post from three years ago.

Registration

With the exam passed and the internship complete, you apply to the state medical council where you intend to practise. Registration is what makes you a doctor in India. It is separate from the college's degree, separate from NMC's list, and separate from the exam.

Keep every document from the Philippines: the degree, the transcript, the internship certificate, the migration certificate, and the NMC and WDOMS screenshots from the year you joined. Registration offices ask for paper, sometimes years later, and a college that has changed its administration may be slow to reissue it.

The Philippine licence, separately

India's registration is one thing. The Philippine licence is another, and it is now open too. Since November 2026 the Philippine Professional Regulation Commission lets Indian graduates of recognised Philippine medical schools sit the Physician Licensure Examination and register to practise in the Philippines. The Embassy of India, Manila confirmed it.

It does not change the Indian route. Whatever licence you hold in the Philippines, you still sit FMGE or NExT and complete the twelve month internship in India to practise here. How the Philippine licence works.

Why degrees fail at the last step

Most failures are not mysterious. They are one of these:

  • The college was not on the list when you joined. A promise that it "will be listed" is not a listing.
  • The course was shorter than 54 months. A fast-track programme creates a registration problem that cannot be fixed after the fact.
  • The internship was somewhere else. The 2026 notice requires it at the same university.
  • Too much of the course was online. The lost time has to be compensated, and a college that does not do it leaves you short.
  • The ten year window ran out. Repeated years and long breaks are not free.
  • The exam was left too late. FMGE is hard, and a failed attempt in the final year is a very expensive way to learn that.
  • The paperwork was incomplete. Missing transcripts and internship certificates delay registration for months.

Questions

Validity questions students ask

Is an MD from the Philippines equal to MBBS in India?

NMC treats the Philippine Doctor of Medicine as equivalent to MBBS for Indian registration, provided the college is listed and you complete the exam and internship. The degree itself is called MD, not MBBS. Do not let the name difference worry you, and do not let a broker use it to charge more.

Can I practise in India without clearing FMGE?

No. Registration requires the screening exam, and the twelve month Indian internship. There is no route around it for a foreign degree.

What if the college is removed from the NMC list after I join?

That is the risk the rules place on the student. The college being listed when you enrol is not a guarantee that it stays listed. Check the list again before you graduate, keep dated screenshots, and ask NMC directly if the status changes. This is one reason to prefer colleges with a long, stable listing.

Does the twelve month internship have to be in India?

For Indian registration, yes. The Philippine internship is part of your MD. The Indian CRMI is a separate requirement after you pass FMGE or NExT.

How many attempts does FMGE allow?

Check the current NBEMS information bulletin. Attempt rules and eligibility have changed before, and a number remembered from an old forum post is not a safe basis for a five year plan.

Is online teaching from the Philippines accepted?

The 2026 notice requires any online teaching to be compensated with equivalent on-campus time. A programme that plans to teach a large share of the course online is a risk, not a shortcut.
